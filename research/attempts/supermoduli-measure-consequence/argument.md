## 1. The restriction lemma [RIG]

This is the single load-bearing mathematical fact behind Proposition C. It is elementary but it is
the entire content of (C1), so it is proved in full rather than asserted.

**Setup.** A supermanifold or superstack $\mathfrak X$ is a ringed space (or stack) with structure
sheaf $\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}$, a sheaf of $\mathbb Z_2$-graded superalgebras on a topological space
$|\mathfrak X|$. Let $\mathcal J\subset\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}$ be the ideal sheaf generated by the
odd elements. The **bosonic truncation** is the ringed space $X:=(|\mathfrak X|,\mathcal
O_{\mathfrak X}/\mathcal J)$; it has the same underlying topological space, $|X|=|\mathfrak X|$. There
is a canonical closed immersion $\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathfrak X$, dual to the surjection of
sheaves $q\colon\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal O_X$.

**Definition (projected).** $\mathfrak X$ is *projected* if there is a morphism
$r\colon\mathfrak X\to X$ with $r\circ\iota=\mathrm{id}_X$. Since $r$ and $\iota$ are both the
identity on the underlying topological space, this is equivalent to a sheaf morphism
$r^\#\colon\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}$ (a superalgebra homomorphism) with
$q\circ r^\#=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal O_X}$, i.e. **a global algebra-sheaf splitting of $q$.**

**Open substacks.** For an open subset $U\subset|X|=|\mathfrak X|$, write $X|_U$ and
$\mathfrak X|_U$ for the open sub-(ringed space/stack) obtained by restricting the structure sheaves
to $U$ ("the open substack of $\mathfrak X$ lying over $U$"). This is the standard notion used
throughout the supergeometry literature this attempt builds on (e.g. [FKP], [CN], [DW] all work with
open substacks of supermoduli stacks in exactly this sense — restriction of the structure sheaf to an
open set of the *same* underlying space). The restriction $q|_U\colon\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}|_U
\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal O_X|_U$ is again the truncation map, and $X|_U\hookrightarrow\mathfrak
X|_U$ is again the closed immersion of the reduced space, now inside $\mathfrak X|_U$.

**Lemma (restriction of projections).** *If $\mathfrak X$ is projected, then $\mathfrak X|_U$ is
projected, for every open $U\subset|X|$.*

*Proof.* Let $r^\#\colon\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}$ be a global splitting of $q$. Sheaf
morphisms restrict to opens: $r^\#|_U\colon\mathcal O_X|_U\to\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}|_U$ is again a
morphism of sheaves of superalgebras (restriction is a functor on sheaves), and
$q|_U\circ r^\#|_U=(q\circ r^\#)|_U=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal O_X}|_U=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal O_X|_U}$
because composition and restriction to an open set commute. So $r^\#|_U$ is a global splitting of
$q|_U$, i.e. a projection $\mathfrak X|_U\to X|_U$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary (the only use made of the lemma here).** Taking $\mathfrak X=\overline{\mathfrak
S}_{g;n,2r}$ and $U$ = the smooth (non-boundary) locus, so that $\mathfrak X|_U=\mathfrak
S_{g;n,2r}$: *projected($\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$) $\Rightarrow$
projected($\mathfrak S_{g;n,2r}$).* Equivalently, by contraposition,
*non-projected($\mathfrak S_{g;n,2r}$) $\Rightarrow$ non-projected($\overline{\mathfrak
S}_{g;n,2r}$)* — but **the converse direction is not a valid inference**: knowing
$\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$ is non-projected (Theorem M) says **nothing** about whether the open
substack $\mathfrak S_{g;n,2r}$ is projected or not; both remain possible. This is exactly (C1).

The same one-line argument applies verbatim to *any* open substack, in particular to a single local
coordinate patch used in a slice-based construction — which is the situation of §3 below.

### 1.1 Independent corroboration: Donagi–Witten already say this

Donagi–Witten, arXiv:1304.7798, Remark 1.4, verbatim (already `L1`-verified — page-image read — at
`literature/extractions/witten-2012-superstring-perturbation.md:601-604`):

> "Though $\mathfrak M_g$ and $\mathfrak M_{g,n}$, like their bosonic counterparts, have natural
> Deligne-Mumford compactifications, we do not consider the compactifications in the present paper.
> Our assertion is that $\mathfrak M_g$ and $\mathfrak M_{g,n}$ are non-projected without any
> consideration of the compactification. **Asking for the compactification to be projected would only
> be a stronger condition.**"

This is precisely the restriction lemma's corollary, stated by the authors whose own methodology (the
covering-space obstruction-transport argument) is the direct ancestor of the elliptic-tail method used
to prove Theorem M. It is worth noting that DW's remark runs in the direction "non-projectedness of
the open locus $\Rightarrow$ non-projectedness of the compactification" (they had only the open-locus
result); Theorem M runs the argument on the compactified side directly (via a boundary test curve),
so the two results are not logically related to each other by this lemma — but the *lemma itself*,
i.e. the general fact that compactified-projected is the strictly stronger condition, is common
ground, stated by DW and re-derived independently above for this attempt.

## 2. Where Theorem M's obstruction actually lives [RIG, reading paper.md]

Theorem M detects $\omega_2(\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r})\ne0$ via a test curve $i(C)\subset
\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$ that is constructed by gluing a moving genus-$h$ factor to a
**constant tail** along a **separating NS node** (paper.md §5–§6; the gluing morphism is FKP's (7.8)).
The test curve, by construction, is a one-parameter family of **stable, nodal** (hence boundary)
supercurves; paper.md states this explicitly at line 189: *"Our non-vanishing class is detected on a
test curve lying inside the NS boundary divisor $\Delta_{\rm NS}$, so Theorem M says nothing whatever
about the smooth locus $\mathfrak S_{g;n,2r}$."* This is the same content as §1's corollary, now
confirmed by the actual geometry of the proof rather than only by the abstract lemma: the obstruction
class Theorem M exhibits is quite literally supported on the boundary, so there is no tension even
before invoking the restriction lemma — the lemma explains *why* this had to be so (a boundary-only
obstruction can never certify anything about the interior), not merely *that* it happens to be so.

Proposition N (paper.md §7) reinforces this from the opposite side: moving the spectator punctures
onto the *interior*-adjacent (moving) component makes the test family affine, and Serre vanishing
forces the obstruction to vanish identically for every choice of odd tangent vector. This is a second,
independent piece of evidence (not merely a corollary of §1's lemma, but a structural fact about this
specific family of constructions) that this method — gluing-at-a-node obstruction detection — is
constitutionally incapable of producing information about the smooth locus. paper.md's own reading of
this (§9.2, its "designed to break the argument" consistency check) is unrelated to physics; the
observation that it also explains (C1) is new to this attempt.

## 3. The mandatory self-check: D'Hoker–Phong's genus-2 program [LIT, L1 + PHYS]

### 3.1 What was computed, and by which method

D'Hoker and Phong, in a six-paper series (2001–2005), constructed the genus-2 two-loop RNS
superstring measure and used it to compute scattering amplitudes:

- **Papers I–IV** (hep-th/0110247, Phys. Lett. B529 (2002) 241; hep-th/0110283, Nucl. Phys. B636
  (2002) 3; hep-th/0111016, Nucl. Phys. B636 (2002) 61; hep-th/0111040, Nucl. Phys. B639 (2002) 129):
  construct a slice-independent, unambiguous formula for the genus-2 chiral superstring measure per
  spin structure, from first principles (not an ansatz), using the super-period matrix as the
  worldsheet-supersymmetry-invariant modulus. As part of this, they show the cosmological constant and
  the $1$-, $2$- and $3$-point functions of massless supergravitons vanish pointwise on moduli space
  for even spin structure (confirmed directly from the abstract of hep-th/0110247, via WebSearch —
  see below for the verification-level caveat).
- **Papers V–VI** (hep-th/0501196, Nucl. Phys. B715 (2005) 91; hep-th/0501197, Nucl. Phys. B715
  (2005) 3): extend the construction to the general $N$-point function (gauge-slice independence,
  Paper V) and give the explicit **four-point** (four-graviton, $n=4$) amplitude together with
  non-renormalization theorems for the $R^4$ term (Paper VI).

**Method, in the authors' own words (Paper II abstract, via WebSearch — verification level noted
below)**: the construction "describes how supergeometries are projected onto their super-period
matrices, and the integration over odd supermoduli is performed by integrating over the fibers of
this projection." This is a first-person confirmation that the method is exactly the one Witten
describes below: fibre integration over a **projection of the (uncompactified, smooth) supermoduli
space**.

**Verification-level note.** The five arXiv IDs, titles and journal citations above were obtained via
`WebSearch` (not by a page-image `Read` of the PDFs), because the load-bearing content of this
argument is the (g,n) *scope* of the series and the *fact* that it uses a projection method — both of
which are corroborated independently and more strongly by the `L1`-verified Witten quotations in §3.2
below. No formula or numerical value from a D'Hoker–Phong paper is used as a premise anywhere in this
argument; only (a) which (g,n,r) they reached, and (b) the qualitative fact that their method rests on
a projection, both of which are cross-confirmed by an independent `L1` source. Per the task's rule,
`WebFetch` of the arXiv abstract pages was attempted first and failed for network-policy reasons (not
content reasons); `WebSearch` was used as the fallback, consistent with the rule that PDF-level
verification is mandatory only for load-bearing formulas/numbers, which these are not.

### 3.2 Witten's own attribution: D'Hoker–Phong rely on uncompactified low-genus projectedness

Witten, arXiv:1209.5461, §3.3.2 "A Hard-To-Avoid Detail," p.35, verbatim (`L1`-verified,
`literature/extractions/witten-2012-superstring-perturbation.md:267-280`):

> "It is rather special for a complex supermanifold to be holomorphically projected or split. There
> has been no reason to believe that the moduli space of super Riemann surfaces has this property, and
> recently it has been shown that this is not the case in general [45]. **(Supermoduli space is
> holomorphically projected in low orders and this fact has been exploited in explicit computations,
> especially in [14].)**"

where [45] = Donagi–Witten (the *uncompactified*, unpunctured non-projectedness theorem, $g\ge5$) and
[14] = D'Hoker–Phong, *Lectures on Two-Loop Superstrings* (the pedagogical review of exactly the
program in §3.1). Witten's definitions of "projected" in this same section (p.35, eq. (3.58)) are for
supermanifolds with **no reference to any compactification** — the object being called "projected in
low orders" is the smooth (uncompactified) supermoduli space, the same object §1's restriction lemma
places entirely outside Theorem M's reach.

A second, independent statement in Donagi–Witten's own paper corroborates this, `L1`-verified at
`literature/extractions/witten-2012-superstring-perturbation.md:614-629` (arXiv:1304.7798, §1, p.4):

> "Physically, if $\mathfrak M_g$ is split — or at least projected — then a possible strategy in
> superstring perturbation theory is to integrate over $\mathfrak M_g$ by first integrating over the
> fibers of its projection to $\mathcal{SM}_g$. **Indeed, practical calculations in superstring
> perturbation theory — such as the $g=2$ calculations that are surveyed in [16] — are usually done in
> this way.** ... The existence of holomorphic projections for small $g$ follows from the cohomological
> nature of the obstructions to splitting and the nature of the reduced spaces $\mathcal{SM}_g$ for
> small $g$, and gives little indication of what happens for larger $g$."

Here [16], in Donagi–Witten's own numbering, is again the D'Hoker–Phong two-loop program (the same
identification is made explicitly in the extraction record). This passage additionally gives the
*mechanism* for why low-genus projectedness of the uncompactified locus holds: DW attribute it to
"the cohomological nature of the obstructions ... and the nature of the reduced spaces $\mathcal
{SM}_g$ for small $g$" — i.e. a low-genus vanishing statement about the obstruction cohomology on the
*smooth* moduli space, structurally the same kind of statement as (but not proved to be, and not
needed to be, the same statement as) Theorem M's boundary-supported non-vanishing on the *compactified*
stack. There is no tension between "obstruction cohomology vanishes on (or near) the smooth locus at
low genus" and "obstruction cohomology does not vanish on the boundary divisor" — they are computed on
different loci of the same space, and §1's lemma is precisely the general statement that neither
implies the other.

### 3.3 Resolution of the self-check

$(g,n,r)=(2,4,0)$, the genus-2 four-point computation of Papers V–VI, satisfies $n+2r=4\ge2$: it is
**numerically inside** the range of Theorem M's Corollary A2 (paper.md: "for every $g\ge2$ and every
$n\ge2$, the even component $\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^+_{g;n,0}$ is non-projected"). $(g,n,r)=(2,0,0)$,
the vacuum-amplitude case of Papers I–IV, is outside Theorem M's own direct reach ($n+2r=0<2$) but
inside the range of the *predecessor* theorem [CN, Thm 1.1(2)], already tabulated in paper.md's own
§1.4 comparison table, which proves the compactified even-component $g=2$ stack (no punctures)
non-projected. Either way, an already-completed, successful, physically load-bearing computation sits
numerically inside a proven compactified non-projectedness statement.

**This is not a contradiction, and Proposition C was not weakened or rewritten because of it**, for
the reason given in §1–§2: D'Hoker–Phong's construction is a statement about, and a use of properties
of, the **uncompactified** locus $\mathfrak S_{2;n,0}$ (they build a slice-independent formula on the
smooth moduli space and separately check its degeneration behaviour at the boundary as a *consistency
requirement on the formula*, not by constructing or requiring a global retraction of the compactified
stack). Theorem M's conclusion is about the **compactified** stack $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{2;n,0}$,
and by the restriction lemma of §1, non-projectedness of the latter is logically silent about the
former. Witten's own attribution (§3.2) independently confirms that the specific property D'Hoker–
Phong's method exploits is projectedness of the *smooth, uncompactified* locus "in low orders" — a
fact about a different, larger object (open substack) than the one Theorem M addresses, and one that
Theorem M's boundary-detected obstruction cannot touch by construction (§2).

**Why this is the valuable part of the check, not a footnote**: it shows that the apparent numerical
overlap between Theorem M's quantifier range and D'Hoker–Phong's completed work is exactly the
situation the restriction lemma predicts is *harmless* — a compactified obstruction supported on the
boundary coexisting with an unconstrained, in-practice-projected smooth locus. Had D'Hoker–Phong's
method instead required a retraction of the *full compactified* stack (including the boundary), the
check would have forced a rewrite of Proposition C; it does not, because — as Donagi and Witten state
plainly in
the passage just quoted — even the theory's own validity "does not depend on a projection," and the
careful (post-2012) treatment of the boundary/degeneration region never assumes one (§4 below).

## 4. The positive content of (C2), and why it is genuinely narrow [PHYS]

### 4.1 What is ruled out, precisely

Theorem M rules out, for every $g\ge3$ and for $g=2$ with $n+2r\ge2$, the existence of a single
global holomorphic retraction of $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$ onto its bosonic truncation — valid
*simultaneously* on the smooth interior and on every boundary stratum (nodal degenerations of every
topological type reachable in codimension $\ge1$). Concretely: nobody could ever have hoped to define
the full genus-$g$, $n$-NS/$2r$-Ramond-puncture superstring measure, including its behaviour at
degeneration limits needed for unitarity/factorization checks, by picking *one* global holomorphic
section of $\mathcal O_{\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}}\to\mathcal O_{X}$ and using it everywhere.
This was already known for the vacuum ($n=r=0$) case at $g=2$ (via [CN]) and for $g\ge3$ unpunctured
(via [CN]); Theorem M's contribution is to show the same holds once **external states are present**,
at $g=2$ with as few as $2$ total NS+Ramond punctures — i.e. already for the lowest-genus,
lowest-point-count external-state amplitudes that are of any physical interest — and, via Corollary A,
that this persists for **every** number of NS punctures with **no** upper bound coupling to $g$, unlike
the DW-ancestor theorems on the uncompactified side (which require $n\le g-1$, paper.md §1.4).

### 4.2 Why the bite is narrow: nobody was using, or needed, that strategy

**[PHYS]** No rigorous construction of the superstring measure in current use assumes or requires a
global compactified projection. Witten's post-2012 framework (arXiv:1209.5461, arXiv:1304.2832)
builds the integration cycle $\Gamma$ from local slices glued by transition data, explicitly
*because* no global splitting can be assumed at any genus, and this design choice predates, and does
not depend on, any specific genus threshold for non-projectedness. (The name "vertical integration"
belongs to the PCO-side systematisation of that gluing, which is **Sen–Witten**, *Filling The Gaps
With PCO's*, arXiv:1504.00609, JHEP **09** (2015) 004 — not to [EW]. Sen–Witten's own abstract
attributes the proposal to earlier work: "Recently, 'vertical integration' was proposed as a
relatively simple method to do this". This project has read that abstract only, so the attribution is
`L2`; see `literature/extractions/witten-2012-superstring-perturbation.md` item EW14.) Donagi and
Witten state the point for the theory's overall logical status, `L1`-verified at the same location as
above:

> "The validity of superstring perturbation theory does not depend on a projection, so the existence
> of string theory gives no hint that $\mathfrak M_g$ is projected."

Since this statement is made about the theory's validity as a whole (integration over the *entire*
moduli space including all its boundary strata, which is exactly the object Theorem M's obstruction is
detected on), it already covers the punctured, compactified case that Theorem M extends into — Theorem
M does not change the *methodology* physicists use, because that methodology was never conditional on
projectedness in the first place, compactified or not, punctured or not. What Theorem M supplies is a
**rigorous closure of a hypothetical retreat position**: even if someone had hoped that, at least for
the very lowest genus and puncture counts relevant to leading-order string phenomenology, a
global-projection shortcut for the boundary sector might be available and might simplify the rigorous
treatment of IR/degeneration effects, Theorem M shows unconditionally that no such shortcut exists,
already at $g=2$ with two external legs. This is real mathematical content — it forecloses a
possibility, not merely fails to open one — but its *physical* significance is limited to confirming
that the general-purpose, patch-based machinery already in use is not merely cautious but strictly
necessary, in a regime where — prior to this paper — that necessity was known only for the vacuum
amplitude ([CN]) and not for any amplitude with external legs.

### 4.3 A sharper, citable structural point: Theorem M confirms a Donagi–Witten expectation

Donagi–Witten's own Remark 1.5 (arXiv:1304.7798, `L1`-verified at
`literature/extractions/witten-2012-superstring-perturbation.md:605-607`), discussing their
*uncompactified*, punctured theorem (which requires $n\le g-1$):

> "Although the upper bound on $n$ in Theorem 1.3 is probably not optimal ... For $n\gg g$, requiring
> the $n$ points to be distinct very likely kills the obstructions to splitness, **though we would
> expect the compactification of $\mathfrak M_{g,n}$ to be non-split.**"

This is a heuristic (their word choice: "very likely", "we would expect"), not a theorem, and it
concerns an asymptotic regime ($n\gg g$) that $(g,n)=(2,4)$ does not obviously belong to. It is quoted
here **not** as a premise but as a second, independent, `L1`-verified textual data point that the
authors closest to this technology anticipated *exactly* the qualitative split that Theorem M now
establishes rigorously in the regime it reaches: many punctures can plausibly restore projectedness on
the **uncompactified** locus (consistent, at the level of expectation, with D'Hoker–Phong's success —
§3), while the **compactified** locus should remain non-projected regardless of how many punctures are
added. Theorem M's Corollary A (paper.md: non-projectedness for *every* $n\ge0$ at $g\ge3$, no
coupling to $g$ at all — the $n\le g-1$ restriction "disappears," paper.md line 178) is precisely a
rigorous, unconditional confirmation of the second half of DW's expectation, in a regime ($g\ge3$, any
$n$; or $g=2$, $n\ge2$) that reaches far beyond DW's own uncompactified $n\le g-1$ ceiling. **This
framing — "Theorem M rigorously realizes an expectation DW record but do not prove" — is, in this
attempt's judgment, the single most citable physical/structural remark available, and is the centrepiece
of `draft-section.md`.**

## 5. What is *not* claimed, and open points

1. **[GAP: uncompactified projectedness at $(g,n)=(2,4)$ is not a theorem, in either direction.**]
   No source found by this attempt proves or disproves projectedness of the uncompactified
   $\mathfrak S_{2;4,0}$. Witten's "projected in low orders" (§3.2) is a qualitative remark
   attributing D'Hoker–Phong's *method* to exploiting projectedness, not a citation of a proved
   theorem with $(g,n)=(2,4)$ as an instance; DW's own Theorem 1.3 stops at $n\le g-1=1$ at $g=2$. This
   attempt does not close this gap and does not need to for Proposition C: (C1) only requires that
   Theorem M's conclusion be *silent* about $\mathfrak S_{2;4,0}$, which the restriction lemma
   guarantees regardless of what the true answer is.
2. **No claim that Theorem M explains any historical difficulty.** A prior attempt in this project
   (`research/attempts/genus-five-supermeasure/proof.md`, §2.1) already investigated and rejected the
   claim that the (unrelated) failure of the genus-$\ge5$ modular-form "ansatz" program is caused by
   any non-projectedness theorem; this attempt is consistent with, and does not revisit, that finding.
   Theorem M is not claimed to explain any past *failure* — only to foreclose a hypothetical future
   shortcut that was never attempted.
3. **No claim of a new obstruction to unitarity, consistency, or any physical prediction.** Nothing
   here touches the content of `graph/statements/consistency/superstring-perturbative-consistency-unproven-1998.yaml`
   or `graph/statements/consistency/massless-tadpole-obstruction-to-superstring-perturbation.yaml`
   (read for context, not cited as premises — this attempt does not write to `graph/`).
4. **[GAP: the patchwise-necessity claim in §4.2 is argued, not derived from Theorem M
   alone.]** The claim that Witten's patch-based machinery is "strictly necessary" in the Theorem-M
   regime rests on combining (a) Theorem M (no global compactified retraction exists) with (b) the
   working assumption that a rigorous treatment of the compactified moduli space's boundary requires
   *either* a global retraction *or* a patch-based construction (no third method is known). (b) is a
   reasonable but not exhaustively justified premise; this attempt has not surveyed the literature for
   a hypothetical third method and does not claim to have ruled one out.
5. **No claim about $r\ge1$ component structure.** paper.md itself (§10.3, [GAP-COMP]) does not
   identify a connected component of $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$ for $r\ge1$; this attempt
   inherits that limitation without comment, since Proposition C does not need component-level
   information (non-projectedness of the whole stack already forecloses a global retraction of the
   whole stack, which is all (C2) needs).

## 6. Verdict

**Outcome type 2**, per the dispatching lead's classification: a real, correctly-quantified, but
narrow and conditional consequence. The mandatory self-check (§3) was executed in full and did
**not** require any weakening or rewrite of Proposition C — the apparent numerical overlap between
Theorem M's range and D'Hoker–Phong's completed computations is resolved cleanly by the restriction
lemma (§1) plus two independent `L1`-verified textual confirmations from Donagi–Witten's own papers
(§3.2), and is corroborated by the D'Hoker–Phong series' own stated method (projection onto the
super-period matrix, §3.1). The most substantive positive content found is §4.3: Theorem M can be read
as a rigorous, unconditional confirmation — in a regime reaching well beyond what Donagi–Witten's own
uncompactified theorem covers — of an expectation Donagi–Witten themselves recorded but did not prove
(Remark 1.5).
