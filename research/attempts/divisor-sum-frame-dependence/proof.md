## 0. Setting, conventions, and the one external input

### 0.1 Lattices and forms

`Λ` denotes an integral even lattice (a free ℤ-module of finite rank with a symmetric bilinear form
`x·y ∈ ℤ` such that `q(x) := x·x ∈ 2ℤ`). No assumption on rank or signature is used. For the physical
application `Λ` is the charge lattice of type II on `K3×T²`, an even unimodular lattice of signature
`(6,22)`.

A **charge pair** is a pair `(Q,P) ∈ Λ²` spanning a rank-2 sublattice `L := ℤQ + ℤP ⊆ Λ` on which the
form is positive definite. A **frame** of `L` is an ordered basis `(Q,P)` of `L`.

A frame determines the integral binary quadratic form

  `f = (m, ℓ, n)`,  `f(x,y) := m x² + ℓ x y + n y² = q(xQ + yP)/2`,
  `m = Q²/2`, `n = P²/2`, `ℓ = Q·P`,

with Gram matrix `G = [[2m, ℓ],[ℓ, 2n]]`, `det G = 4mn − ℓ² =: Δ > 0`, and
`disc f = ℓ² − 4mn = −Δ`. Forms are written `(a,b,c)` for `ax²+bxy+cy²`. The **content** is
`c(f) := gcd(m,ℓ,n)`.

Changing the frame by `A = [[a,b],[c,d]] ∈ GL(2,ℤ)`, `(Q,P) ↦ (aQ+bP, cQ+dP)`, replaces `f` by
`f∘Aᵀ`. Thus frames of `L` correspond to forms in the `GL(2,ℤ)`-class of `f`, and oriented frames
(S-duality, `SL(2,ℤ)`) to the `SL(2,ℤ)`-class. Two even positive-definite rank-2 lattices are
isometric iff their forms are `GL(2,ℤ)`-equivalent; `GL(2,ℤ)`-classes are `SL(2,ℤ)`-classes modulo
the involution `(a,b,c) ↦ (a,−b,c)`, which is inversion in the form class group.

`L^∨ := {v ∈ L⊗ℚ : v·L ⊆ ℤ}`; `D_L := L^∨/L` is the discriminant group, `|D_L| = Δ`, carrying the
quadratic form `q̄ : D_L → ℚ/2ℤ`, `q̄(v+L) = q(v) mod 2ℤ`. A subgroup `H ⊆ D_L` is **isotropic** if
`q̄|_H ≡ 0`.

### 0.2 The object under study

For a frame with `I²|m` and `I|ℓ` the literature (DGM eq. (52); Theorem 6.1 of the paper under
study) forms

  **`D_I(m,n,ℓ) := Σ_{s|I} s · d_*(m/s², n, ℓ/s)`**  (★)

and asserts that it computes the single-centred (immortal) index of a 1/4-BPS dyon of torsion
`I = gcd(Q∧P)`. Remark 6.3 of the paper exhibits one lattice and two frames on which (★) takes two

## 1. Overlattices, admissible frames, and the subgroup `H(Q)`

### Lemma 1.0 (primitive lifts)

Let `I ≥ 1` and let `(x̄, ȳ) ∈ (ℤ/I)²` satisfy `gcd(x̄, ȳ, I) = 1`. Then there is a **primitive**
vector `(x,y) ∈ ℤ²` (i.e. `gcd(x,y) = 1`) reducing to `(x̄, ȳ)` mod `I`.

*Proof.* If `I = 1` take `(1,0)`. Otherwise pick any integer lifts `x, y`; adding `I` to `x` if
necessary, assume `x ≠ 0`. Put `e := gcd(x, I)`. Since `e | x` and `e | I`, the hypothesis
`gcd(x,y,I) = 1` forces `gcd(y, e) = 1`. We look for `t ∈ ℤ` with `gcd(x, y + It) = 1`. Let `q` be a
prime with `q | x` and `q | y + It`. If `q | I` then `q | e`, and `q | y + It` gives `q | y`,
contradicting `gcd(y,e) = 1`; so `q ∤ I`, i.e. `I` is invertible mod `q`, and the condition
`y + It ≡ 0 mod q` singles out exactly one residue class of `t` mod `q`. The set of primes `q | x`
with `q ∤ I` is finite, and for each we must avoid one class out of `q ≥ 2`; by the Chinese remainder
theorem such a `t` exists. Then `gcd(x, y+It) = 1` and `(x, y+It) ≡ (x̄,ȳ) mod I`. ∎

### Lemma 1.1 (when the `s`-th summand is a lattice)

Let `(Q,P)` be a frame of `L` with form `(m,ℓ,n)` and let `s ≥ 1`. Put
`N_s := ℤ(Q/s) + ℤP ⊆ L⊗ℚ`. Then

  `N_s` is an even lattice containing `L` with `[N_s : L] = s`  ⟺  `s² | m` and `s | ℓ`.

Moreover in that case `L ⊆ N_s ⊆ L^∨`, and the form of `N_s` in the frame `(Q/s, P)` is
`(m/s², ℓ/s, n)`.

*Proof.* `N_s ⊇ L` because `Q = s(Q/s)`, and `N_s/L` is generated by `(Q/s)+L`, which has order
exactly `s` because `k(Q/s) = (k/s)Q ∈ L = ℤQ ⊕ ℤP` iff `s | k`. Hence `[N_s:L] = s`. The form of
`N_s` in the basis `(Q/s, P)` has coefficients `q(Q/s)/2 = m/s²`, `(Q/s)·P = ℓ/s`, `q(P)/2 = n`;
`N_s` is integral and even iff these three are integers, i.e. iff `s²|m` and `s|ℓ` (`n ∈ ℤ` always).
Finally, if `s²|m` and `s|ℓ` then `(Q/s)·Q = 2m/s ∈ ℤ` and `(Q/s)·P = ℓ/s ∈ ℤ`, so `Q/s ∈ L^∨`, and
`P ∈ L ⊆ L^∨`; hence `N_s ⊆ L^∨`. ∎

**Definition 1.2.** A frame `(Q,P)` of `L` is **`I`-admissible** if `I²|m` and `I|ℓ`, i.e. iff the
summands of (★) are all defined as lattices. For an `I`-admissible frame put

  `H(Q) := ⟨ (Q/I) + L ⟩ ⊆ D_L`.

### Lemma 1.3 (even overlattices ↔ isotropic subgroups)

The assignment `N ↦ N/L` is an inclusion-preserving bijection from the set of even lattices `N` with
`L ⊆ N ⊆ L⊗ℚ` onto the set of isotropic subgroups of `D_L`, with inverse `H ↦ π⁻¹(H)` where
`π : L^∨ → D_L`.

*Proof.* If `N ⊇ L` is even and integral then for `v ∈ N`, `w ∈ L ⊆ N` we get `v·w ∈ ℤ`, so
`N ⊆ L^∨` and `N/L ⊆ D_L` is defined; `q̄(v+L) = q(v) ≡ 0 mod 2ℤ` since `N` is even, so `N/L` is
isotropic. Conversely let `H ⊆ D_L` be isotropic and `N := π⁻¹(H) ⊆ L^∨`. For `v ∈ N`,
`q(v) ∈ 2ℤ` by isotropy; for `v,w ∈ N`, `v+w ∈ N`, so
`v·w = (q(v+w) − q(v) − q(w))/2 ∈ ℤ`. Hence `N` is an even lattice. The two maps are mutually
inverse because `L = π⁻¹(0)`. ∎

### Lemma 1.4 (the subgroup is the whole story)

Let `(Q,P)` be an `I`-admissible frame of `L`. Then:

1. `H(Q)` is a **cyclic isotropic subgroup of `D_L` of order exactly `I`**;
2. for every `s | I`, the unique subgroup of `H(Q)` of order `s` is `⟨(Q/s) + L⟩`, and the even
   overlattice it corresponds to under Lemma 1.3 is exactly `N_s = ℤ(Q/s) + ℤP`;
3. consequently `N_s` is determined by the pair `(L, H(Q))` alone, with no reference to the frame.

*Proof.* (1) Order exactly `I` was shown in Lemma 1.1 (with `s=I`). Isotropy: for `k ∈ ℤ`,
`q̄(k(Q/I)+L) = q(kQ/I) = 2k²m/I² ∈ 2ℤ` since `I²|m`. (Membership `Q/I ∈ L^∨` is Lemma 1.1.)
(2) `H(Q)` is cyclic of order `I` generated by `ξ := (Q/I)+L`, so its unique order-`s` subgroup is
generated by `(I/s)ξ = (Q/s)+L`. Its preimage under `π` is `ℤ(Q/s) + L = ℤ(Q/s) + ℤP = N_s` (using
`Q = s(Q/s)`).
(3) Immediate from (2) and the bijectivity in Lemma 1.3. ∎

### Theorem A (Frame Structure Theorem)

Let `L` be an even positive-definite rank-2 lattice, `I ≥ 1`, and let `(Q,P)` and `(Q',P')` be two
`I`-admissible frames of `L` with `H(Q) = H(Q')`. Then for every `s | I`

  `ℤ(Q/s) + ℤP = ℤ(Q'/s) + ℤP'`  **as subsets of `L⊗ℚ`** (not merely isometric),

and hence, writing `(m,ℓ,n)` and `(m',ℓ',n')` for the two forms, **[uses I1]**

  `Σ_{s|I} s·d_*(m/s², n, ℓ/s) = Σ_{s|I} s·d_*(m'/s², n', ℓ'/s)`.

*Proof.* By Lemma 1.4(2) both sides of the displayed equality of lattices are `π⁻¹(H_s)`, where
`H_s` is the unique subgroup of order `s` of `H(Q) = H(Q')`. Equal lattices are isometric, so by
(I1) the corresponding values of `d_*` agree term by term. ∎

**Definition 1.5.** `𝒞_I(L) := {cyclic isotropic subgroups of `D_L` of order exactly `I`}`. By
Theorem A there is a well-defined function **[uses I1]**

  `𝔡_I : 𝒞_I(L) → ℤ`,  `𝔡_I(H) := Σ_{s|I} s·d_*(π⁻¹(H_s))`,

and every `I`-admissible frame `(Q,P)` satisfies `D_I(m,n,ℓ) = 𝔡_I(H(Q))`.

### Proposition 1.6 (which subgroups occur; the fibres)

1. The map `Φ : {I\text{-admissible frames of } L} → 𝒞_I(L)`, `(Q,P) ↦ H(Q)`, is **surjective**.
2. Every element of `D_L` of order dividing `I` lies in `(1/I)L/L ≅ L/IL ≅ (ℤ/I)²`. Consequently
   `𝒞_I(L)` is a subset of the set of order-`I` cyclic subgroups of `(ℤ/I)²`, which is `ℙ¹(ℤ/I)`,
   of cardinality `ψ(I) = I∏_{p|I}(1+1/p)`. Explicitly, identifying `[x:y] ∈ ℙ¹(ℤ/I)` with
   `⟨(xQ+yP)/I + L⟩` for any frame `(Q,P)`,

     `𝒞_I(L) = { [x:y] ∈ ℙ¹(ℤ/I) : G·(x,y)ᵀ ≡ 0 mod I and f(x,y) ≡ 0 mod I² }`,

   where the second congruence is well defined once the first holds.
3. `Φ` is equivariant for the action of `SL(2,ℤ)` on frames, and the fibres of `Φ` are the orbits of
   `Γ⁰(I) := {[[a,b],[c,d]] ∈ SL(2,ℤ) : b ≡ 0 mod I}` (a conjugate of `Γ₀(I)` by
   `S = [[0,−1],[1,0]]`, of index `ψ(I)`).

*Proof.* (2) If `ξ ∈ D_L` has `Iξ = 0`, a representative `v ∈ L^∨` satisfies `Iv ∈ L`, i.e.
`v ∈ (1/I)L`; so `ξ ∈ (1/I)L/L`, and `(1/I)L/L ≅ L/IL ≅ (ℤ/I)²` via `v ↦ Iv`. Writing
`ξ = (xQ+yP)/I + L`, the subgroup `⟨ξ⟩` has order exactly `I` iff `gcd(x,y,I) = 1`, i.e. iff `(x,y)`
is a unimodular row mod `I`; and `⟨ξ⟩ = ⟨ξ'⟩` iff `(x,y)` and `(x',y')` differ by a unit of `ℤ/I`.
The set of such classes is `ℙ¹(ℤ/I)`, of size `ψ(I)`. Now `ξ ∈ L^∨` iff `(xQ+yP)·Q/I ∈ ℤ` and
`(xQ+yP)·P/I ∈ ℤ`, i.e. iff `G·(x,y)ᵀ ≡ 0 mod I`. Given that, replacing `(x,y)` by `(x+Iu, y+Iv)`
changes `f(x,y)` by `I·((x,y)G(u,v)ᵀ) + I²f(u,v) ≡ 0 mod I²`, so `f(x,y) mod I²` depends only on
`[x:y]`; and `q̄(ξ) = 2f(x,y)/I² ∈ 2ℤ` iff `I² | f(x,y)`, which by Lemma 1.4(1) is the isotropy
condition.
(1) Given `[x̄:ȳ] ∈ 𝒞_I(L)`, choose by **Lemma 1.0** a primitive lift `(x,y) ∈ ℤ²`, and complete it
to a frame `(Q',P')` of `L` with `Q' := xQ+yP` (possible exactly because `(x,y)` is primitive). Then
`m' = f(x,y) ≡ 0 mod I²` and `ℓ' = Q'·P' ≡ 0 mod I` (because `Q'/I ∈ L^∨` and `P' ∈ L`), so the
frame is `I`-admissible, and `H(Q') = ⟨Q'/I + L⟩ = [x̄:ȳ]`.
(3) `H(aQ+bP) = H(Q)` iff `(a,b) ≡ u(1,0)` mod `I` for a unit `u`, i.e. iff `b ≡ 0 mod I` (then
`ad ≡ 1 mod I` forces `a` to be a unit). ∎

**Remark 1.7.** Proposition 1.6(2) shows `|𝒞_I(L)| ≤ ψ(I)`, and `𝒞_I(L)` is computed by congruences
mod `I` and `I²` from any single frame: it is a purely local object. §3 computes it completely for
`I` prime.

## 2. The invariant object

Throughout this section `L ⊆ Λ` is a rank-2 positive-definite sublattice of the even lattice `Λ`,
and `M := (L⊗ℚ) ∩ Λ` is its **saturation**: the smallest primitive sublattice of `Λ` containing `L`.

### Lemma 2.1 (torsion = saturation index; `I² | Δ`)

`[M:L] = gcd(Q∧P) =: I` for any frame `(Q,P)` of `L`, where `gcd(Q∧P)` is the content of
`Q∧P ∈ ⋀²Λ`. Moreover `M` is an even overlattice of `L` with `M ⊆ L^∨`, so `M/L ⊆ D_L` is isotropic
and `[M:L]² | Δ`; in particular `I² | Δ`.

*Proof.* `Λ/M` is finitely generated and torsion-free, hence free, so `M` is a direct summand of `Λ`;
choosing a basis `(e,f)` of `M` and extending to a basis of `Λ` shows `e∧f` is primitive in `⋀²Λ`.
Writing `Q = ae+bf`, `P = ce+df` gives `Q∧P = (ad−bc)\,e∧f`, so `gcd(Q∧P) = |ad−bc| = [M:L]`.
`M ⊆ Λ` is even (sublattice of an even lattice) and `M·L ⊆ Λ·Λ ⊆ ℤ` gives `M ⊆ L^∨`; by Lemma 1.3,
`M/L` is isotropic. Finally, for any lattice `N ⊇ L` of index `k` in the same rational span, the
Gram matrices satisfy `det(Gram L) = k²·det(Gram N)`, so `det(Gram M) = Δ/I²`; since `M` is an
integral lattice this is a (positive) integer, i.e. `I² | Δ`. ∎

### Definition 2.2 (the invariant)

  **`𝒟(L ⊆ Λ) := Σ_{N} [N:L] · d_*(N)`**, the sum over all rank-2 sublattices `N ⊆ Λ` with
  `L ⊆ N`.

The sum is finite: any such `N` satisfies `N ⊆ (L⊗ℚ) ∩ Λ = M`, so the `N` are exactly the
preimages of the subgroups of `M/L`, a finite group of order `I`. Equivalently

  `𝒟(L ⊆ Λ) = Σ_{H ⊆ M/L} |H| · d_*(π⁻¹(H))`.

### Proposition 2.3 (invariance) **[uses I1]**

`𝒟(L ⊆ Λ)` depends only on the pair `(L ⊆ Λ)`. In particular it is invariant under change of frame
of `L` (S-duality), and `𝒟(gL ⊆ Λ) = 𝒟(L ⊆ Λ)` for every `g ∈ O(Λ)` (T-duality).

*Proof.* Definition 2.2 mentions no basis, so the first two assertions are immediate. For `g ∈ O(Λ)`,
`N ↦ gN` is a bijection between the intermediate lattices for `L` and those for `gL`, preserving
indices, and `d_*(gN) = d_*(N)` by (I1) since `gN ≅ N`. ∎

### Theorem B (adapted frames = the frames that see only charge lattices)

Let `(Q,P)` be an `I`-admissible frame of `L`, `I = [M:L]`. The following are equivalent:

  (i) `Q ∈ IΛ` (the frame is **adapted**, in the terminology of the paper);
  (ii) `N_s ⊆ Λ` for every `s | I`;
  (iii) `N_I = M`;
  (iv) `H(Q) = M/L`.

Each implies that `M/L` is cyclic of order `I`. Conversely, if `M/L` is cyclic of order `I` then
adapted frames exist, and every adapted frame is `I`-admissible.

*Proof.*
(i)⇒(iii): `Q = Iw` with `w ∈ Λ`; then `w ∈ (L⊗ℚ) ∩ Λ = M`, so `N_I = ℤw + ℤP ⊆ M`; both have index
`I` over `L` (Lemma 1.1 and Lemma 2.1), so `N_I = M`.
(iii)⇒(ii): `N_s ⊆ N_I` because `Q/s = (I/s)(Q/I) ∈ N_I`; and `N_I = M ⊆ Λ`.
(ii)⇒(i): `N_I ⊆ Λ` gives `Q/I ∈ Λ`.
(iii)⇔(iv): Lemma 1.3 (the preimage map is injective on subgroups) plus Lemma 1.4(2).
Any of them gives `M/L = H(Q)`, cyclic of order `I` by Lemma 1.4(1).
Conversely suppose `M/L = ⟨w+L⟩` is cyclic of order `I`. Then `Iw ∈ L`, say `Iw = x₀Q₀+y₀P₀` for a
frame `(Q₀,P₀)`, and `gcd(x₀,y₀,I) = 1` since `w+L` has order `I`. By Lemma 1.0 choose
`(x,y) ≡ (x₀,y₀) mod I` with `gcd(x,y) = 1`, and set `Q := xQ₀+yP₀`; since `(x−x₀, y−y₀) ∈ Iℤ²` we
have `Q = Iw + Iv` with `v ∈ L`, so `Q/I = w+v ∈ M` and `Q/I + L = w + L` still generates `M/L`.
Complete `Q` to a frame `(Q,P)` of `L` (possible as `(x,y)` is primitive). Then `Q/I ∈ M ⊆ Λ`, so
the frame is adapted; and `Q/I ∈ M ⊆ L^∨` with `q(Q/I)/2 ∈ ℤ` (`M` even) gives `I²|m`, `I|ℓ`, i.e.
`I`-admissibility. The same computation shows any adapted frame is `I`-admissible. ∎

### Corollary 2.4 (the divisor sum computes the invariant, on adapted frames) **[uses I1]**

If `M/L` is cyclic of order `I`, then for **every** adapted frame `(Q,P)` of `L`

  `D_I(m,n,ℓ) = Σ_{s|I} s·d_*(m/s², n, ℓ/s) = 𝒟(L ⊆ Λ)`.

*Proof.* By Theorem B, `H(Q) = M/L`, so by Lemma 1.4(2) the lattices `N_s` (`s|I`) are exactly the
preimages of the subgroups of the cyclic group `M/L ≅ ℤ/I`, one for each `s|I`, with `[N_s:L] = s`.
Hence the two sums coincide term by term with Definition 2.2. ∎

### Corollary 2.5 (the premise `(P'_ad)` is frame-free)

Let `(P'_ad)` be the physical premise of the paper under study — "the attractor-contour integral
computes the single-centred degeneracy `d_{ic}(Q,P)`, asserted for adapted frames" — and let
`(P_inv)` be the statement

  `d_{ic}(Q,P) = 𝒟(L ⊆ Λ)`.

If `M/L` is cyclic then `(P'_ad) ⟺ (P_inv)`.

*Proof.* Immediate from Corollary 2.4: on an adapted frame the two right-hand sides are the same
integer, and adapted frames exist (Theorem B). ∎

> **Honest reading of Corollary 2.5.** This is a *translation*, not a proof: it does not establish
> either premise. What it does establish is that the adaptedness restriction introduced by the paper
> is not an extra physical hypothesis — it is the coordinate description of a coordinate-free
> quantity, and its S-duality invariance, which had to be *imposed* in the frame language, is
> automatic in the invariant language (Proposition 2.3). Note also that `(P_inv)` has content in
> exactly the cases where `(P'_ad)` has: Theorem B shows adapted frames exist iff `M/L` is cyclic.

### Proposition 2.6 (what a non-adapted admissible frame computes)

Let `(Q,P)` be an `I`-admissible frame with `H(Q) ≠ M/L` (in particular this happens whenever
`|𝒞_I(L)| ≥ 2`, for all but at most one of the subgroups). Then `N_I ⊄ Λ`: the `s = I` summand of
(★) is `d_*` of a lattice that is not a lattice of charges.

*Proof.* If `N_I ⊆ Λ` then `N_I ⊆ (L⊗ℚ) ∩ Λ = M` and `[N_I:L] = I = [M:L]` force `N_I = M`, i.e.
`H(Q) = M/L`. ∎

### Proposition 2.7 (uniqueness of the invariant, and the limits of uniqueness)

Among all functions `F` of the pair `(L ⊆ Λ)`, the condition "`F(L ⊆ Λ) = D_I(m,n,ℓ)` for some (hence
every) adapted frame" determines `F` uniquely on the set of pairs with `M/L` cyclic, and there
`F = 𝒟`. On pairs with `M/L` non-cyclic the condition is vacuous and does **not** determine `F`;
Definition 2.2 is then a choice, justified only by naturality. See §7 and [GAP-NONCYCLIC].

*Proof.* Immediate from Corollary 2.4 and Theorem B. ∎

## 3. `𝒞_I(L)` explicitly: a purely local computation

By Proposition 1.6(2), `𝒞_I(L)` is cut out of `ℙ¹(ℤ/I)` by congruences. CRT on `ℙ¹(ℤ/I) ≅
∏_{p^a‖I} ℙ¹(ℤ/p^a)` splits both congruences prime by prime, giving

### Lemma 3.1 (multiplicativity)

`𝒞_I(L) ≅ ∏_{p^a ‖ I} 𝒞_{p^a}(L)`; in particular `𝒞_I(L) ≠ ∅` iff `𝒞_{p^a}(L) ≠ ∅` for every
`p^a ‖ I`. ∎

Write `c := c(f)` for the content, `f = c·f₁` with `f₁` primitive of discriminant
`D₁ = −Δ/c²`, and for a prime `p` set `α := v_p(c)` and `β := v_p(Δ) − α = α + v_p(D₁)`.

### Theorem D (the size of `𝒞_p(L)`)

Let `p` be a prime.

1. **(odd `p` only)** If `α = 0`: `|𝒞_p(L)| = 0` when `β ≤ 1`, and `|𝒞_p(L)| = 1` when `β ≥ 2`.
   **This statement is false at `p = 2`** (494 counterexamples with `Δ ≤ 600`, the smallest being
   `f = (1,0,1)`, `Δ = 4`, `β = 2`, `𝒞_2 = ∅`); the sharp statement at `p = 2` is (4).
2. If `α = 1` (any `p`, including `2`): `|𝒞_p(L)| = 1 + χ_{D₁}(p)`, where `χ_{D₁}` is the Kronecker
   symbol: `2` if `p` splits in the order `𝒪_{D₁}`, `0` if `p` is inert, `1` if `p` ramifies.
3. If `α ≥ 2` (any `p`): `𝒞_p(L) = ℙ¹(𝔽_p)`, so `|𝒞_p(L)| = p+1`.
4. Unconditional `p = 2` statement, valid for every `α`: `𝒞_2(L) = ∅` if `ℓ` is odd; otherwise
   `𝒞_2(L) = {[x:y] ∈ ℙ¹(𝔽₂) : f(x,y) ≡ 0 mod 4}`, so
   `|𝒞_2(L)| = #{v ∈ {m, n, m+ℓ+n} : 4 | v}`.

*Proof.* The conditions are `G·(x,y)ᵀ ≡ 0 mod p` and `f(x,y) ≡ 0 mod p²`.

(4) `G mod 2 = [[0,ℓ],[ℓ,0]]`, so the first condition reads `ℓy ≡ ℓx ≡ 0 mod 2`: it fails for all
`[x:y] ∈ ℙ¹(𝔽₂)` if `ℓ` is odd and is vacuous if `ℓ` is even. In the latter case the second
condition evaluated at the three points `[1:0], [0:1], [1:1]` gives `m, n, m+ℓ+n ≡ 0 mod 4`. No
assumption on `α` was used.

(3) `p^α | c` with `α ≥ 2` gives `p² | f(x,y)` for all `(x,y)`, and `p | c` divides `2m, ℓ, 2n`, so
`G ≡ 0 mod p`: both conditions are vacuous.

(2) `α = 1`: again `p | c` makes the condition `G ≡ 0 mod p` vacuous. The second condition is
`c f₁(x,y) ≡ 0 mod p²` with `v_p(c) = 1`, i.e. `f₁(x,y) ≡ 0 mod p`. Since `f₁` is primitive,
`f₁ mod p` is a nonzero binary quadratic form over `𝔽_p`, and the number of its zeros in `ℙ¹(𝔽_p)`
is `1 + χ_{D₁}(p)`. For odd `p` this is the classical count
`#{t : f₁(t,1) ≡ 0} + [p | m₁] = 1 + (D₁/p)`. For `p = 2` check the three points directly: if
`D₁ ≡ 1 mod 8` then `ℓ₁` is odd and `4m₁n₁ = ℓ₁² − D₁ ≡ 0 mod 8`, so `m₁n₁` is even and exactly two
of `m₁, n₁, m₁+ℓ₁+n₁` are even; if `D₁ ≡ 5 mod 8` then `m₁n₁` is odd and all three are odd; if
`D₁ ≡ 0 mod 4` then `ℓ₁` is even, `m₁, n₁` are not both even (primitivity), and exactly one of the
three is even. These are `2, 0, 1 = 1 + χ_{D₁}(2)`.

(1) `α = 0`, `p` odd. If `p ∤ Δ` (i.e. `β = 0`) then `det(G mod p) = Δ ≢ 0`, so `G mod p` is
invertible and there is no nonzero solution. If `p | Δ`, then `G mod p` is singular but nonzero:
`G ≡ 0 mod p` would give `p | 2m, ℓ, 2n`, hence (`p` odd) `p | c`, contradicting `α = 0`. So
`G mod p` has rank exactly `1` and its kernel is a single point of `ℙ¹(𝔽_p)`. Choose a frame in
which that point is `[1:0]`: then `p | 2m` and `p | ℓ`, so `p | m`, and `p ∤ n` (else `p | c`). The
remaining condition is `p² | f(1,0) = m`. Finally, using `p | ℓ` and `p ∤ n`:

 - if `v_p(m) = 1` then `v_p(4mn) = 1` while `v_p(ℓ²) ≥ 2`, so `β = v_p(Δ) = v_p(4mn − ℓ²) = 1`;
 - if `v_p(m) ≥ 2` then `v_p(4mn) ≥ 2` and `v_p(ℓ²) ≥ 2`, so `β ≥ 2`.

Hence `p² | m ⟺ β ≥ 2`, which is the assertion. ∎

> **Why (1) fails at `p = 2`.** The step "`p | 2m ⟹ p | m`" uses `p` odd. At `p = 2` the kernel
> condition only gives `2 | ℓ` and `2 | 2m`, which is no information about `m`; correspondingly
> `f = (1,0,1)` has `β = 2` but `𝒞_2 = ∅`, since `x² + y² ≡ 0 mod 4` has no primitive solution. This
> is why (4) is stated separately and why the `p = 2` column of every table below is computed from
> (4), never from (1).
>
> *Verification*: claim (4) was checked against the definition on all 15159 reduced forms of
> discriminant `≥ −2000` (`scripts/experiments.py tables`, 0 failures); (1)–(3) for `p = 3,5,7` on
> the same range; the failure of (1) at `p = 2` is exhibited in `scripts/experiments.py esteps`.

### Corollary 3.2 (when the literature formula is safe)

If `|𝒞_I(L)| ≤ 1` then (★) is independent of the admissible frame, and (given `M/L` cyclic) equals
`𝒟(L ⊆ Λ)`. By Theorem D and Lemma 3.1 this happens, for example, whenever `c(f)` is squarefree and
coprime to `I`. ∎

## 4. Refutation of the "class number 1" criterion

§9 of the paper under study asserts that the frame dependence "is invisible **exactly when** the
relevant discriminants `Δ/s²` have class number 1". We settle both directions. Two readings of
"class number `h(D)`" must be distinguished:

  - **(P)** the standard one, `h(D) = |Pic(𝒪_D)| =` number of `SL(2,ℤ)`-classes of **primitive**
    forms of discriminant `D`;
  - **(T)** the total one, `h_tot(D) =` number of `GL(2,ℤ)`-classes of **all** integral
    positive-definite forms of discriminant `D` `=` number of isometry classes of even
    positive-definite rank-2 lattices of determinant `−D`.

### Theorem C1 (the true half, and only under reading (T)) **[uses I1]**

If for every `s | I` with `s > 1` we have `h_tot(−Δ/s²) = 1`, then (★) takes the same value on all
`I`-admissible frames of `L`.

*Proof.* For any `H ∈ 𝒞_I(L)` and `s | I`, `N_s(H)` is an even lattice of determinant
`Δ/s²` (Lemma 1.1: index `s` over `L`). If there is only one isometry class of such lattices, then
`d_*(N_s(H))` is independent of `H` by (I1); sum over `s`. ∎

### Theorem C2 (the claim is false under the standard reading (P), in **both** directions)

1. *(the "if" half fails under (P))* `Δ = 64`, `f = (4,0,4)`, `I = 2`: `Δ/4 = 16` and `h(−16) = 1`
   (the only primitive class is `(1,0,4)`), yet (★) takes two values:
   `7999541469408` and `7999235715072`. This is the paper's own witness. Two further cases with
   `Δ ≤ 400`: `Δ = 112, f = (4,4,8), I = 2` and `Δ = 243, f = (9,9,9), I = 3`.
2. *(the "only if" half fails under (P) and under (T))* `Δ = 60`, `f = (2,2,8)`, `I = 2`:
   `h(−15) = h_tot(−15) = 2`, `|𝒞_2(L)| = 2` (two genuinely different admissible frames), yet both
   give the same value `30312332797600`. Likewise `Δ = 180`, `f = (6,6,9)`, `I = 3`:
   `h(−20) = h_tot(−20) = 2`, `|𝒞_3(L)| = 2`, single value `168030151116840500544`.

*Proof.* The integers are computed in `scripts/dstar_witnesses.py` (exact integer arithmetic; the
`d_*` implementation is the one of the `torsion-immortal-positivity-hecke` attempt, which reduces
every form before reading the DMZ finite-part coefficient). For (1),
`D_2 = d_*(4,4,0) + 2 d_*(1,4,0) = 7999169992704 + 2·185738352` in the frames with
`H = ⟨(1,0)⟩, ⟨(0,1)⟩`, and `D_2 = d_*(8,4,8) + 2 d_*(2,4,4) = 7999169992704 + 2·32861184` in the
frame with `H = ⟨(1,1)⟩`; the chains of isometry classes are `[(4,0,4),(1,0,4)]` and
`[(4,0,4),(2,0,2)]`. The class `(2,0,2) = 2·(1,0,1)` is imprimitive, which is exactly why `h(−16)=1`
fails to see it. For (2) the two admissible subgroups give the same chain of classes
`[(2,2,8),(2,1,2)]` resp. `[(6,6,9),(1,0,5)]` — see Theorem E below for why. ∎

**Remark 4.1 (how big the failure of "only if" is).** A scan over all isometry classes of even
positive-definite rank-2 lattices with `Δ ≤ 400` and all `2 ≤ I ≤ 6` (541 pairs `(L,I)` possessing at
least one admissible frame; `scripts/experiments.py scan`) gives:

| | some `h_tot > 1` | all `h_tot = 1` |
|---|---|---|
| frame **dependent** | 42 | **0** |
| frame **independent** | 447 | 52 |

The `0` is Theorem C1. The `447` is the failure of "only if": 48 of them even have `|𝒞_I(L)| ≥ 2`,
so several genuinely different admissible frames exist and all give the same value. The smallest are
`Δ=48, f=(4,4,4), I=2` (`|𝒞_2| = 3`, one value) and `Δ=60, f=(2,2,8), I=2`. Under reading (P) the
"if" half fails as well (3 cases with `Δ ≤ 400`, including the paper's witness).

### Theorem C3 (the correct criterion, in computable form)

(★) takes the same value on all `I`-admissible frames of `L` **if** the map

  `κ : 𝒞_I(L) → ∏_{s|I} \{isometry classes\}`,  `H ↦ ([N_s(H)])_{s|I}`,

is constant. The converse holds unless `d_*` takes equal values on two non-isometric lattices of the
same determinant — see [GAP-INJ]. `κ` is computable from `(m,ℓ,n,I)` by a finite exact computation
(Proposition 1.6(2) for the domain, Gauss reduction for the target), so this criterion is decidable.

*Proof.* Sufficiency is (I1) applied termwise; decidability is the content of Proposition 1.6(2) and
the finiteness of reduction. ∎

§§5–6 evaluate `κ` in closed form for `I = p` prime, which is where the qualitative answer to "what
is the criterion really about" lives.

## 5. `I = p` with `v_p(content) = 1`: a class-**group** criterion

This is the regime `α = 1`. By Theorem D, `|𝒞_p(L)| = 2` exactly when `p` splits in `𝒪_{D₁}`,
which is the only case in which anything can go wrong.

### Theorem E

Let `p` be a prime, `f = c·f₁` with `f₁` primitive of discriminant `D₁`, `α := v_p(c) = 1`, and
suppose `p ∤ D₁` and `p` splits in `𝒪_{D₁}` (so `|𝒞_p(L)| = 2`). Write `c = p c'` and let `𝔭` be
either of the two prime forms of discriminant `D₁` above `p`. Then the two values of
`κ` are

  `{ [N_p(H)] : H ∈ 𝒞_p(L) } = { c'·([f₁][𝔭]), c'·([f₁][𝔭]⁻¹) }`

as classes of forms of discriminant `c'²D₁` (the product being taken in the form class group
`Cl(D₁) ≅ Pic(𝒪_{D₁})`). Consequently

  **(★) is independent of the admissible frame ⟺ `[𝔭]² = 1` or `[f₁]² = 1` in `Pic(𝒪_{D₁})`.**

*Proof.* Fix `H ∈ 𝒞_p(L)`, i.e. a zero `[x:y]` of `f₁ mod p` (Theorem D(2)), and choose a frame with
first vector `xQ+yP` (Proposition 1.6(1)); in it the form is `g = c g₁` with
`g₁ = (m₁, ℓ₁, n₁)` in the `SL(2,ℤ)`-class of `f₁` and `p | m₁`. By Lemma 1.1 the form of `N_p` is

  `(m/p², ℓ/p, n) = (c m₁/p², c ℓ₁/p, c n₁) = c'·h`,  `h := (m₁/p, ℓ₁, p n₁)`,  `disc h = D₁`.

*`h` is primitive*: if `p | c(h)` then `p² | m₁` and `p | ℓ₁`, whence `p² | ℓ₁² − 4m₁n₁ = D₁`,
contradicting `p ∤ D₁`. (For `p = 2`: `4 | m₁`, `2 | ℓ₁` give `4 | D₁`, same contradiction.)

*Composition*: let `𝔭 := (p, ℓ₁, m₁n₁/p)`, an integral form of discriminant
`ℓ₁² − 4m₁n₁ = D₁` (integral because `p | m₁`), primitive (if `p | c(𝔭)` then `p | ℓ₁`, hence
`p ∤ 4m₁n₁` because `p ∤ D₁`, contradicting `p | m₁`). Set `a₁ := m₁/p`, `a₂ := p`, `b := ℓ₁`,
`e := n₁`. Then `h = (a₁, b, a₂e)` and `𝔭 = (a₂, b, a₁e)`, and
`gcd(a₁,a₂,b) = gcd(m₁/p, p, ℓ₁) = 1` (else `p² | m₁` and `p | ℓ₁`, excluded as above). Dirichlet
composition of united forms (valid for any discriminant `≡ 0,1 mod 4` and any primitive forms in
this position) gives

  `[h]·[𝔭] = [(a₁a₂, b, e)] = [(m₁, ℓ₁, n₁)] = [g₁] = [f₁]`,

so `[h] = [f₁][𝔭]⁻¹`.

*The second zero gives the conjugate prime*: in the chosen frame `f₁ mod p ≡ y(ℓ₁x + n₁y)` (using
`p|m₁`), and `p ∤ ℓ₁` (else `p | D₁`), so the two zeros are `[1:0]` and `[−n₁ : ℓ₁]`. Pick an integer
lift `(x,y) = (x, ℓ₁)` of the second zero with `x ≡ −n₁ mod p` and `gcd(x, ℓ₁) = 1` (possible: for
each prime `q | ℓ₁` we have `q ≠ p`, so `p` is invertible mod `q` and the condition
`x ≢ 0 mod q` excludes one residue mod `q`; apply CRT). Complete to a frame with `(u,v)` satisfying
`xv − ℓ₁u = 1`. The new middle coefficient is

  `ℓ₁' = 2m₁xu + ℓ₁(xv + ℓ₁u) + 2n₁ℓ₁v ≡ ℓ₁(1 + 2ℓ₁u) + 2n₁ℓ₁v = ℓ₁ + 2ℓ₁(ℓ₁u + n₁v) mod 2p`,

using `p | m₁`. From `xv − ℓ₁u = 1` and `x = −n₁ + tp` we get `ℓ₁u + n₁v = tpv − 1`, so
`ℓ₁' ≡ ℓ₁ − 2ℓ₁ + 2pℓ₁tv ≡ −ℓ₁ mod 2p`. Since a form `(p, b, ·)` of discriminant `D₁` is determined
up to `SL(2,ℤ)`-class by `b mod 2p`, and `(p,b,·)⁻¹ = (p,−b,·)`, the prime attached to the second
zero is `[𝔭]⁻¹`. Hence the second value of `κ` is `c'([f₁][𝔭])`.

> *Independent check of the two steps just made* (they are the only places in Theorem E where an
> explicit computation replaces a citation): `scripts/experiments.py esteps` recomputes, for all
> primitive `f₁` of discriminant `≥ −600` and all `p ∈ {2,3,5,7,11,13}` with `p ∤ D₁` and two zeros,
> (i) the two middle coefficients `ℓ₁, ℓ₁'` and (ii) the two prime forms, and verifies
> `ℓ₁' ≡ −ℓ₁ mod 2p` and "second prime = conjugate of the first". **5694 cases, 0 violations of
> either.**

*The criterion*: the two classes `u := c'[f₁][𝔭]⁻¹` and `w := c'[f₁][𝔭]` give the same isometry
class iff they are equal or inverse (`GL` vs `SL`); multiplication by the constant `c'` is injective
on classes and commutes with inversion, so this reads `[f₁][𝔭]⁻¹ = [f₁][𝔭]` (i.e. `[𝔭]² = 1`) or
`[f₁][𝔭]⁻¹ = [f₁]⁻¹[𝔭]⁻¹` (i.e. `[f₁]² = 1`). By Theorem C3 this is exactly the criterion for
frame-independence of `κ`; for the value of (★) see [GAP-INJ]. ∎

### Corollary 5.1 (the class **number** is irrelevant)

In the regime of Theorem E, frame-independence is a condition on the *elements* `[f₁]` and `[𝔭]` of
`Pic(𝒪_{D₁})` (being 2-torsion, i.e. ambiguous), not on the *size* of that group. In particular
`h(D₁)` can be arbitrarily large with (★) frame-independent, and `h(D₁) = h > 1` occurs both with and
without frame dependence.

*Verification*: `scripts/experiments.py criterion` tests the equivalence of Theorem E against the
directly computed `κ` for `p ∈ {2,3,5,7,11,13}` and all `L` with `Δ ≤ 3000`, `v_p(c) = 1`,
`|𝒞_p| = 2`: **1798 cases, 0 mismatches**. Among them `h(D₁)` reaches 31 in the frame-independent
rows and 31 in the frame-dependent rows. A minimal illustration: at `Δ = 207`, `h(−23) = 3`, the
two lattice classes `f = (3,3,18)` (`[f₁] = [(1,1,6)] = 1`, ambiguous) and `f = (6,3,9)`
(`[f₁] = [(2,−1,3)]`, of order 3) have the *same* `Δ`, the *same* `D₁`, the *same* class number, the
*same* `|𝒞_3| = 2` — and the first is frame-independent while the second is frame-dependent, with the
two integer values `4315653393243150656664` and `4315653393219717545088` (computed in
`scripts/dstar_witnesses.py`; for `(3,3,18)` only the class chain, not the integer, is within the
tabulated `1/Φ₁₀` range, but the `Δ = 180` case `f = (6,6,9)` gives an explicit integer witness of
the independent side: one value `168030151116840500544` from both frames, although `h(−20) = 2`).

### Corollary 5.2 (the failure is invisible to every genus invariant)

In the regime of Theorem E the two competing classes differ by `[𝔭]²`, a square in
`Pic(𝒪_{D₁})`. By the principal genus theorem (Gauss), classes differing by a square lie in the
same genus. Hence the two lattices `N_p(H₁)`, `N_p(H₂)` are isomorphic over `ℤ_q` for every prime
`q` and over `ℝ`: no local invariant, no genus symbol, no `p`-adic Jordan decomposition can detect
the frame dependence. It is a purely global, class-group phenomenon.

*(This corollary is interpretive; the principal genus theorem is used nowhere else and no statement
above depends on it.)*

## 6. `I = p` with `v_p(content) ≥ 2`: the Hecke correspondence, and why the paper's own
   witness lives here

This is the regime `α ≥ 2`, where `𝒞_p(L) = ℙ¹(𝔽_p)` is as large as possible. Note that the
paper's witness `f = (4,0,4)`, `p = 2` has `c = 4`, `α = 2`, so it is **not** covered by Theorem E.

### Theorem E′

Let `α = v_p(c(f)) ≥ 2` and write `f = c f₁` with `f₁` primitive. Identify `𝒞_p(L) = ℙ¹(𝔽_p)`
(Theorem D(3)). Under this identification, for `[x:y] ∈ ℙ¹(𝔽_p)`,

  `[N_p([x:y])] = (c/p²)·[f₁ ∘ A_{[x:y]}]`,

where `A_{[x:y]}` is the matrix whose columns are a primitive lift `(x,y)` and `p·(u,v)` with
`xv − yu = 1`; i.e. `f₁ ∘ A_{[x:y]}` is the form of the index-`p` sublattice
`ℤ(x e + y f) + p L₁ ⊆ L₁` of the primitive lattice `L₁` with form `f₁`. Thus the map `κ` is the
classical `p`-Hecke correspondence `[f₁] ↦ \{` the `p+1` index-`p` sublattice classes `\}`, scaled by
`c/p²`.

*Proof.* In the frame with first vector `xQ+yP` the form is `g = c g₁` with `g₁ = f₁∘A` for an
`A ∈ SL(2,ℤ)` with first column `(x,y)`, and by Lemma 1.1 the form of `N_p` is

  `(m/p², ℓ/p, n) = (c m₁/p², c ℓ₁/p, c n₁) = (c/p²)·(m₁, p ℓ₁, p² n₁) = (c/p²)·(g₁ ∘ diag(1,p))`,

and `g₁ ∘ diag(1,p) = f₁ ∘ (A·diag(1,p)) = f₁ ∘ A_{[x:y]}`. All coefficients are integers because
`p² | c`. ∎

### Corollary 6.1 (a sharp dichotomy at `α ≥ 2`)

With `α ≥ 2`: the number of zeros of `f₁ mod p` in `ℙ¹(𝔽_p)` is `1 + χ_{D₁}(p) ∈ \{0,1,2\}`, and
`[x:y]` is a zero iff the sublattice `f₁∘A_{[x:y]}` is **imprimitive**. Since `p+1 ≥ 3 > 2` there is
always at least one non-zero point. Hence:

  - if `p` is **not inert** in `𝒪_{D₁}` (i.e. `χ_{D₁}(p) ≥ 0`), then `κ` takes at least two values,
    distinguished already by the **content**, and (★) is frame-**dependent** (subject to
    [GAP-INJ] only for the passage from `κ` to the integer (★));
  - if `p` is **inert**, all `p+1` sublattices are primitive of discriminant `p²D₁` and
    frame-independence holds iff they all lie in one `GL(2,ℤ)`-class.

*Proof.* The form of `ℤ(xe+yf) + pL₁` is `(f₁(x,y), p·(…), p²·(…))`, whose content is divisible by
`p` iff `p | f₁(x,y)`. Content is an isometry invariant, so a primitive and an imprimitive
sublattice are never isometric. ∎

**Example 6.2 (the paper's witness, explained).** `f = (4,0,4) = 4·(1,0,1)`, `p = 2`, `α = 2`,
`f₁ = (1,0,1)`, `D₁ = −4`, and `2` ramifies in `ℤ[i]`. The three index-2 sublattices of `ℤ²` are
`2ℤ⊕ℤ`, `ℤ⊕2ℤ` (forms `(1,0,4)` after reduction, primitive) and `ℤ(1,1)+2ℤ²` (form `(2,0,2)`,
imprimitive). Corollary 6.1 predicts frame dependence, with exactly two values, the discriminating
invariant being the *content* of the index-2 overlattice — not its class in `Pic(𝒪_{−16})`, which
is trivial. This is precisely why the paper's `h(−16) = 1` reading fails: `h` counts only primitive
classes, and the witness is a primitive-vs-imprimitive phenomenon.

**Example 6.3 (three values).** `f = (4,0,8) = 4·(1,0,2)`, `p = 2`, `Δ = 128`: the three index-2
sublattices of the `(1,0,2)`-lattice have classes `(2,0,4)` (imprimitive; this is the zero of
`f₁ mod 2`, `2` being ramified in `𝒪_{−8}`), `(1,0,8)` and `(3,2,3)` — three distinct classes, so
(★) takes **three** distinct values on `L`. Three is the maximum observed over all `Δ ≤ 400`,
`I ≤ 6`; it is attained exactly at `Δ = 128, 192, 256` with `f = (4,0,8), (4,0,12), (4,0,16)`.

*Verification*: `scripts/experiments.py alpha2` checks Theorem E′ and Corollary 6.1 against the
directly computed `𝒞_p` and `κ` for `p ∈ {2,3,5}` over all forms with `Δ ≤ 2000` and
`v_p(c) ≥ 2`. Result: `p=2`, 220 forms, 0 mismatches, 182 of them have a zero of `f₁ mod 2` and
**all 182 are frame-dependent**; `p=3`, 19 forms, 0 mismatches, 14 with a zero, all 14 dependent;
`p=5`, 1 form, 0 zeros (the range `Δ ≤ 2000` barely reaches `α=2` at `p=5`, which needs
`Δ ≥ 5⁴·3`).

## 7. A second, sharper failure: non-cyclic saturation quotient

Theorem B shows adapted frames exist **iff `M/L` is cyclic**. This is a real restriction: if
`M ⊆ Λ` is primitive and `L = kM` then `M/L ≅ (ℤ/k)²` and `I = [M:L] = k²`.

### Proposition 7.1

Let `M ⊆ Λ` be primitive of rank 2 with form `f₀`, let `L := 2M`, so `I = 4` and
`M/L ≅ (ℤ/2)²`. Then:

1. No adapted frame exists; the premise `(P'_ad)` of the paper is **vacuous** for such charges.
2. `𝒞_4(L) ≠ ∅` iff `f₀` primitively represents a multiple of 4; when this holds, (★) *is* defined
   in some frame, and it has the wrong **shape**: it has 3 terms with weights `1,2,4` (total 7),
   whereas the number of rank-2 lattices `N` with `L ⊆ N ⊆ Λ` is 5, with weights `1,2,2,2,4`
   (total 11). So (★) cannot equal `𝒟(L ⊆ Λ)` unless by accident.
3. (★) can moreover be frame-dependent here as well: for `f₀ = (1,0,4)` (`Δ(L) = 256`),
   `|𝒞_4(L)| = 2` and the two admissible subgroups give different chains
   `[(4,0,16),(4,0,4),(1,0,4)]` and `[(4,0,16),(4,0,4),(2,0,2)]`. Since the physical degeneracy is a
   function of the charge pair, (★) is **certainly wrong** for that charge, independently of any
   conjecture about what the right answer is.

*Proof.* (1) Theorem B. (2) `𝒞_4(L)` is given by Proposition 1.6(2); with `f = 4f₀` the condition
`G ≡ 0 mod 4` is `2G₀ ≡ 0 mod 4`, i.e. automatic up to the `ℓ`-parity condition, and
`f(x,y) ≡ 0 mod 16` reads `f₀(x,y) ≡ 0 mod 4`. The counts of terms are Definition 2.2 (subgroups of
`(ℤ/2)²`: one of order 1, three of order 2, one of order 4) versus the divisors of 4.
(3) Direct computation, `scripts/experiments.py noncyclic`. ∎

**Remark 7.2 (how common this is).** Such configurations are realizable in the physical charge
lattice: every even positive-definite rank-2 lattice embeds primitively into an even unimodular
lattice of signature `(6,22)` (Nikulin's criterion: `rank Λ − rank M = 26 > 2 ≥ ℓ(D_M)`), and
`Q = 2e`, `P = 2f` is then a legitimate charge pair of torsion 4. The experiment finds 236 lattices
`M` with `Δ(M) ≤ 200` for which `L = 2M` admits a 4-admissible frame. (The Nikulin input is used
only for this realizability remark.)

**Consequence for the paper under study.** Its restriction to adapted frames is not merely a
convenient normalization: it silently restricts the *theorem* to charges with cyclic `M/L`. That
restriction should be stated as a hypothesis, and the complementary set is not small.

## 8. Summary of the answers

**Q1 — is "invisible exactly when `h(Δ/s²) = 1`" true?** No.
 - Under the standard reading of `h` (primitive classes), **both** directions fail; the "if"
   direction fails on the paper's own witness (Theorem C2(1)).
 - Under the generous reading `h_tot` (all classes), the "if" direction is true and easy
   (Theorem C1), and the "only if" direction fails massively: 447 of 489 cases with `Δ ≤ 400`,
   `I ≤ 6` and `h_tot > 1` are frame-independent (Remark 4.1).
 - The correct criterion is Theorem C3 (computable in general), which in the two prime regimes
   becomes: a **class-group** condition `[f₁]² = 1 ∨ [𝔭]² = 1` when `v_p(c) = 1` (Theorem E); and a
   **content/Hecke** condition when `v_p(c) ≥ 2` (Corollary 6.1). The class *number* is irrelevant
   (Corollary 5.1), and the phenomenon is invisible to all local invariants (Corollary 5.2).

**Q2 — what is the correct S-duality-invariant object?**
`𝒟(L ⊆ Λ) = Σ_{L ⊆ N ⊆ Λ} [N:L]·d_*(N)`, the index-weighted sum over the finitely many rank-2
lattices between `L` and its saturation (Definition 2.2). It is manifestly a function of the pair
`(L ⊆ Λ)` and hence S- and T-duality invariant (Proposition 2.3); it equals the divisor sum (★) in
every adapted frame (Corollary 2.4); and the adapted-frame premise is equivalent to the frame-free
premise `(P_inv)` (Corollary 2.5). The divisor sum over `s|I` is the shadow of `𝒟` in a frame, and is
a function of the pair `(L, H)` with `H ∈ 𝒞_I(L)` — `(m,n,ℓ,I)` remembers `L` and `I` but not `H`
(Theorem A). Adapted frames are exactly the frames with `H = M/L`, equivalently the frames in which
every lattice whose `d_*` is being evaluated is an honest sublattice of `Λ` (Theorem B,
Proposition 2.6).

## 9. Gaps

- **[GAP-I1]** Everything mentioning `d_*` is conditional on Input (I1) (`d_*` is a `GL(2,ℤ)`-class
  function), which is itself at epistemic status `physical-argument` in the graph. The lattice
  theory (§§1–3, §5–§7 minus the `d_*` sentences) is unconditional.
- **[GAP-INJ]** Theorem C3, Theorem E and Corollary 6.1 characterize when the *chain of isometry
  classes* `κ` is constant. Frame-independence of the *integer* (★) is implied, but the converse
  requires that `d_*` separate non-isometric lattices of equal determinant. This is not known. All
  computed examples separate them (e.g. `d_*(1,4,0) = 185738352 ≠ 32861184 = d_*(2,2,0)`), but no
  proof or general mechanism is offered here. *This is a genuine gap, not a formality: without it,
  "frame-dependent" statements are statements about `κ`, and only the "frame-independent" direction
  is a statement about the number.*
- **[GAP-NONCYCLIC]** When `M/L` is not cyclic there is no adapted frame, `(P'_ad)` is vacuous, and
  Definition 2.2 still produces a well-defined invariant `𝒟(L ⊆ Λ)` — but **nothing here derives
  that `𝒟` is the physically correct single-centred degeneracy in that case**. Proposition 2.7 makes
  the non-uniqueness explicit. Deciding it requires going back to the attractor-contour derivation,
  which is outside the scope of this attempt. What *is* established unconditionally is that the
  literature formula (★) cannot be right there (Proposition 7.1(3)).
- **[OPEN-PRIMEPOWER]** `𝒞_{p^a}(L)` and the map `κ` are computed here only for `a = 1` (Theorems D,
  E, E′). The general prime-power case is a finite local computation of the same type; nothing above
  claims it. General `I` reduces to prime powers by Lemma 3.1.
- **[OPEN-INERT]** In Corollary 6.1 the inert case is reduced to "do the `p+1` index-`p` sublattices
  of `L₁` lie in one class?", which is the classical Hecke/Brandt question; no closed form is given
  here.
