# Claim: is the $(g;n,2r)$ threshold of the compactified-punctured non-projectedness theorem sharp?

**Slug:** `supermoduli-threshold-sharpness`
**Opened:** 2026-09-23
**Object under study:** the draft manuscript
`research/papers/supermoduli-punctured-nonprojected/paper.md`, whose Corollary 8.5 reads

$$\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}\ \text{is non-projected whenever}\quad g\ge 3,\ \ \text{or}\ \ (g=2\ \text{and}\ n+2r\ge 2).$$

The question this attempt answers is whether that region is **sharp**, i.e. whether the complementary
region

$$\mathcal R^{c}\ :=\ \{g=2,\ n+2r\le 1\}\ \cup\ \{g=1,\ \text{all }(n,r)\}\ \cup\ \{g=0,\ \text{all }(n,r)\}$$

consists of stacks that are genuinely projected/split, or merely of stacks the paper's construction
cannot reach.

---

## 0. Notation and standing conventions

All notation is that of `paper.md` §2, repeated here so that this file is self-contained.

- $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$: the moduli superstack of **stable supercurves** of genus $g$ with
  $n$ Neveu–Schwarz and $2r$ Ramond punctures, in the sense of Felder–Kazhdan–Polishchuk
  ([FKP] = *The moduli space of stable supercurves and its canonical line bundle*, Amer. J. Math.
  **145** (2023) 1777–1886, arXiv:2006.13271). By [FKP, Thm A] it is a **smooth proper
  Deligne–Mumford superstack** over $\mathbb C$.
- $\mathfrak S_{g;n,2r}\subset\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$: the open substack of **smooth**
  supercurves. This is DW's $\mathfrak M_{g,n}$ when $r=0$.
- $\overline S_{g;n,2r}:=(\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r})_{\rm bos}$: the bosonic truncation, the
  stack of stable spin curves with $n$ NS and $2r$ Ramond punctures (`paper.md` Lemma 5.1).
- $\mathcal J\subset\mathcal O_{\overline{\mathfrak S}}$: the ideal generated by odd functions;
  $\mathcal T_\pm$ the even/odd parts of $\mathcal T\mathfrak X/\mathcal J\mathcal T\mathfrak X$;
  $\mathcal T_+\cong\mathcal T_X$, $\mathcal T_-\cong(\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2)^\vee$.
- **Projected**: the closed embedding $X\hookrightarrow\mathfrak X$ admits a retraction
  $\mathfrak X\to X$. **Split**: $\mathfrak X\cong$ the total space of an odd vector bundle on $X$.
  Split $\Rightarrow$ projected.
- $\omega_2(\mathfrak X)\in H^1(X,\mathcal T_X\otimes\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee)$: Green's primary
  obstruction ([CN, Prop. 2.2] = `paper.md` Prop. 4.1).
- **Odd dimension.** $\dim\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}=(3g-3+n+2r\mid 2g-2+n+r)$; we write
  $$d(g,n,r):=2g-2+n+r=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal T_-.$$
- **Stability.** $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$ is non-empty iff $2g-2+n+2r>0$.
- $[\mathrm{CN}]$ = Corrêa–Noja, arXiv:2606.06733; $[\mathrm{DW}]$ = Donagi–Witten, arXiv:1304.7798v2;
  $[\mathrm{DO}]$ = Donagi–Ott, arXiv:2308.07957; $[\mathrm{FKP2}]$ = arXiv:2408.11136.

## 1. Claims proved in `proof.md`

> **Claim A (automatic splitness in odd rank $\le1$).**
> Let $\mathfrak X$ be a smooth Deligne–Mumford superstack over $\mathbb C$ with
> $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal T_-\mathfrak X\le1$. Then $\mathfrak X$ is **canonically split**
> (hence projected).
>
> **Consequence A$'$.** $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$ is split whenever $2g-2+n+r\le1$, i.e. for
> exactly the following stable triples:
> $$(g,n,r)\in\{(1,1,0),\,(1,0,1)\}\ \cup\ \{(0,n,r):n+2r\ge3,\ n+r\le3\},$$
> the latter set being $(0;3,0),(0;2,1),(0;1,1),(0;1,2),(0;0,2),(0;0,3)$.

> **Claim B (non-projectedness propagates from open substacks).**
> Let $\mathfrak X$ be a smooth DM superstack and $\mathfrak U\subseteq\mathfrak X$ an open substack.
> If $\mathfrak X$ is projected (resp. split) then so is $\mathfrak U$. Equivalently: if $\mathfrak U$
> is non-projected then $\mathfrak X$ is non-projected.
>
> (This is the implication [DW, Remark 1.4] states verbatim — *"Asking for the compactification to be
> projected would only be a stronger condition"* — but which `paper.md` never uses.)

> **Claim C (the threshold of `paper.md` is not sharp: the whole $g=2$ row falls).**
> For **every** $g\ge2$, every $n\ge0$ and every $r\ge0$,
> $$\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}\ \text{ is non-projected.}$$
> Moreover for $r=0$ the **even** parity component $\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{+}_{g;n,0}$ is
> non-projected for every $g\ge2$ and every $n\ge0$.
> The two cells outside `paper.md`'s box are supplied by the published literature, not by a new
> argument: $(g,n,r)=(2,0,0)$ by K4, and $(g,n,r)=(2,1,0)$ by K3 together with Claim B.

> **Claim D (a genuinely split case with odd rank $2$).**
> $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;4,0}$, of dimension $(1\mid2)$, is **split**. Concretely,
> $\omega_2(\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;4,0})=0$ because its bosonic truncation is a $\mu_2$-gerbe over
> the $\mu_2$-root stack $\mathcal X$ of $(\mathbb P^1,\,0+1+\infty)$, on which
> $$\deg_{\mathcal X}\mathcal T_{\mathcal X}=\tfrac12,\qquad
> \deg_{\mathcal X}\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee=\tfrac12,\qquad
> \deg_{\mathcal X}\big(\mathcal T\otimes\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee\big)=1>\deg\omega_{\mathcal X},$$
> so $H^1$ of the obstruction sheaf vanishes by Serre duality; K2 then upgrades $\omega_2=0$ to
> splitness because $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal T_-=2$.

> **Claim E (what stays open).** After A–D the region $\mathcal R^c$ decomposes as
> * **closed, non-projected:** $g=2$, all $(n,r)$ — Claim C;
> * **closed, split:** $2g-2+n+r\le1$ — Claim A; plus $(g,n,r)=(0,4,0)$ — Claim D; plus
>   $\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^-_{2;0,0}$ — K4;
> * **open:** $g=1$ with $n+r\ge2$; $g=0$ with $n+r\ge4$ other than $(4,0)$; and, at the level of
>   parity components, $\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^-_{2;n,0}$ for $n\ge1$.

## 2. What is *not* claimed

1. No claim that $g\le1$ is entirely split. Claims A and D cover only finitely many cells of it.
2. Claim C asserts non-projectedness of the **whole stack** for $r>0$, and of the **even component**
   only for $r=0$; nothing about the odd component at $g=2$ with $n\ge1$.
3. Nothing is claimed about $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;n,0}$ for $n\ge5$ or about
   $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;n,2r}$ with $r\ge1$ and $n+r\ge4$.
4. Claims C and E rest on the correctness of `paper.md` Cor. 8.5 (K5) only for the cells the paper
   itself covers; Claims A, B, D are independent of it.
