## 1. Automatic splitness in odd rank $\le 1$

The first observation is that a whole corner of the complementary region $\mathcal R^c$ is split for a
reason having nothing to do with supermoduli: the odd directions are too few to carry an obstruction.

**Lemma 1.1.** *Let $\mathfrak X$ be a smooth Deligne–Mumford superstack over $\mathbb C$, let
$\mathcal J\subset\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}$ be the ideal generated by the odd sections, and let
$X=\mathfrak X_{\rm bos}$ be the closed substack it defines. If
$q:=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal T_-\mathfrak X\le1$, then*
$$\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}\ =\ \mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar0}\ \oplus\ \mathcal J,
\qquad \mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar 0}=\mathcal O_X ,$$
*canonically as sheaves of $\mathbb Z_2$-graded $\mathcal O_X$-algebras; in particular $\mathfrak X$ is
**split**, hence projected.*

*Proof.* If $q=0$: smoothness means that étale-locally $\mathfrak X\cong\mathbb A^{p|q}_{\;}$-charts,
so $q=0$ forces $\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}$ to be purely even and $\mathcal J=0$; then
$\mathfrak X=X$ and there is nothing to prove.

Assume $q=1$. Smoothness gives an étale chart on which $\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}$ is a free
$\mathcal O$-algebra on one odd generator $\theta$; there $\mathcal J=(\theta)$ and
$\mathcal J^2=(\theta^2)=0$. The condition $\mathcal J^2=0$ is étale-local, hence holds on
$\mathfrak X$.

Now use the canonical $\mathbb Z_2$-grading
$\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}=\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar0}\oplus\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar1}$.

1. $\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar1}\subseteq\mathcal J$, because $\mathcal J$ is by definition the ideal
   generated by odd sections.
2. Conversely $\mathcal J=\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}\cdot\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar1}
   =\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar0}\cdot\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar1}
   +\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar1}\cdot\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar1}$, and the second summand is
   contained in $\mathcal J\cdot\mathcal J=0$. Hence
   $\mathcal J=\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar0}\cdot\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar1}
   \subseteq\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar1}$.

Combining 1 and 2, $\mathcal J=\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar1}$ exactly. Therefore
$\mathcal O_X=\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}/\mathcal J=\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar0}$, and the inclusion
$\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X,\bar0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}$ is a morphism of sheaves of
algebras splitting the quotient map. Dually this is a retraction $\mathfrak X\to X$, so $\mathfrak X$
is projected.

For splitness, put $\mathcal F:=\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2=\mathcal J$, a locally free $\mathcal O_X$-module
of rank $1$, placed in odd degree. Since $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal F=1$ we have
$\Lambda^2_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal F=0$, so
$\Lambda^\bullet_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal F=\mathcal O_X\oplus\mathcal F$. The decomposition
$\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}=\mathcal O_X\oplus\mathcal J$ obtained above is an isomorphism onto
$\Lambda^\bullet\mathcal F$ of sheaves of $\mathbb Z_2$-graded $\mathcal O_X$-algebras: both sides have
the same underlying module, and the products agree because $\mathcal J\cdot\mathcal J=0=\Lambda^2\mathcal F$.
Thus $\mathfrak X\cong\operatorname{Tot}(\Pi\mathcal F)$ is split. Every step used only the canonical
grading, so the isomorphism is canonical and requires no gluing argument — it is therefore valid on a
Deligne–Mumford stack verbatim. $\square$

> **Remark 1.2 (why this is not just "$\omega_2$ lands in the zero group").** When $q\le1$ one has
> $\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-=0$, so $\omega_2$ trivially vanishes. But vanishing of the *primary*
> obstruction alone does not imply projectedness in general (only in rank $2$, by K2). Lemma 1.1
> gives the conclusion directly and unconditionally, with no obstruction theory at all.

**Corollary 1.3.** *$\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$ is split whenever $d(g,n,r)=2g-2+n+r\le1$.
Among stable triples ($2g-2+n+2r>0$) these are exactly*
$$(g;n,2r)\ \in\ \{(1;1,0),\ (1;0,2)\}\ \cup\
\{(0;3,0),\ (0;2,2),\ (0;1,2),\ (0;1,4),\ (0;0,4),\ (0;0,6)\}.$$

*Proof.* By K1 the stack is smooth, and $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal T_-=d(g,n,r)$ is the odd dimension.
Apply Lemma 1.1. The enumeration is the finite check performed by `scripts/region.py`:

* $g\ge2$ gives $d\ge2$ always, so no triple with $g\ge2$ occurs.
* $g=1$: $d=n+r$, and stability is $n+2r\ge1$; $d\le1$ leaves $(n,r)=(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$.
* $g=0$: $d=n+r-2$, stability is $n+2r\ge3$; $d\le1$ means $n+r\le3$, leaving
  $(n,r)=(3,0),(2,1),(1,1),(1,2),(0,2),(0,3)$.

(The two $g=1$ entries are the familiar $\mathfrak M_{1,1}$ and its two-Ramond-puncture analogue;
the six $g=0$ entries include the $(0\mid0)$-dimensional $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;1,2}$ and
$\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;0,4}$, which are ordinary stacks.) $\square$

## 2. Non-projectedness propagates from open substacks to the compactification

**Lemma 2.1.** *Let $\mathfrak X$ be a smooth DM superstack and $\mathfrak U\subseteq\mathfrak X$ an
open substack, with bosonic truncations $X$ and $U=\mathfrak U_{\rm bos}$. If $\mathfrak X$ is
projected then $\mathfrak U$ is projected; if $\mathfrak X$ is split then $\mathfrak U$ is split.
Contrapositively: if $\mathfrak U$ is non-projected then so is $\mathfrak X$.*

*Proof.* Smoothness gives $\mathcal J^{\,q+1}=0$ with $q=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal T_-<\infty$, so
$\mathcal J$ is nilpotent and the closed immersion $\iota:X\hookrightarrow\mathfrak X$ induces a
**homeomorphism** $|\iota|:|X|\to|\mathfrak X|$ of underlying topological spaces. Open substacks of
$\mathfrak X$ (resp. $X$) are in bijection with open subsets of $|\mathfrak X|$ (resp. $|X|$).

Let $\rho:\mathfrak X\to X$ satisfy $\rho\circ\iota=\mathrm{id}_X$. Then
$|\rho|\circ|\iota|=\mathrm{id}_{|X|}$, and since $|\iota|$ is a homeomorphism,
$|\rho|=|\iota|^{-1}$ is one too. Write $\mathfrak U$ for the open substack with
$|\mathfrak U|=W\subseteq|\mathfrak X|$; then $|U|=|\iota|^{-1}(W)$ and
$$|\rho^{-1}(U)|=|\rho|^{-1}\big(|\iota|^{-1}(W)\big)=|\iota|\big(|\iota|^{-1}(W)\big)=W=|\mathfrak U| ,$$
so $\rho^{-1}(U)=\mathfrak U$ as open substacks. Hence $\rho$ restricts to
$\rho_{\mathfrak U}:\mathfrak U\to U$, and $\rho_{\mathfrak U}\circ\iota_U=\mathrm{id}_U$ because it is
the restriction of $\rho\circ\iota=\mathrm{id}_X$ to an open substack. So $\mathfrak U$ is projected.

If $\mathfrak X\cong\operatorname{Tot}(\Pi E)$ for an odd bundle $E$ on $X$, then
$\mathfrak U\cong\operatorname{Tot}(\Pi E|_U)$, so splitness restricts as well. $\square$

> **Remark 2.2 (this is not new; it is only unused).** [DW, Remark 1.4] states the same implication
> verbatim: *"Though $\mathfrak M_g$ and $\mathfrak M_{g,n}$, like their bosonic counterparts, have
> natural Deligne–Mumford compactifications, we do not consider the compactifications in the present
> paper. Our assertion is that $\mathfrak M_g$ and $\mathfrak M_{g,n}$ are non-projected without any
> consideration of the compactification. **Asking for the compactification to be projected would only
> be a stronger condition.**"* (L1-verified transcription recorded in
> `literature/extractions/witten-2012-superstring-perturbation.md`, entry EW21.)
>
> `paper.md` §9.3 correctly notes the *converse* failure ("non-projectedness of the compactification
> does not imply non-projectedness of the smooth locus") and correctly declines to claim any
> improvement on [DW]/[DO]/[W] *as statements about $\mathfrak S_{g;n,2r}$*. But it never runs the
> implication in the direction that does hold, and that direction is exactly what closes the one
> genuinely interesting hole in its threshold. See §3.

## 3. The threshold of `paper.md` is not sharp: the entire $g=2$ row is non-projected

**Proposition 3.1.** *$\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{+}_{2;1,0}$ is non-projected. Consequently
$\overline{\mathfrak S}_{2;1,0}$ is non-projected.*

*Proof.* $\mathfrak S^{+}_{2;1,0}$, the open substack of $\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{+}_{2;1,0}$
consisting of **smooth** supercurves of genus $2$ with one NS puncture and even spin structure, is
precisely Donagi–Witten's $\mathfrak M^{+}_{g,1}$ at $g=2$; this identification of names is the one
already used by [CN] and adopted verbatim in `paper.md` §2.2
($\mathfrak M^+_{h,1}:=\mathfrak S^+_{h;1,0}$). By K3 = [DW, Thm 1.2] — verbatim *"The supermanifold
$\mathfrak M_{g,1}$ is non-projected for $g\ge2$, for the case of an even spin structure"* — the
stack $\mathfrak S^{+}_{2;1,0}$ is non-projected.

By K1 the compactification $\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{+}_{2;1,0}$ is a smooth DM superstack and
$\mathfrak S^{+}_{2;1,0}$ is an open substack of it. Lemma 2.1 (contrapositive) gives
$\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{+}_{2;1,0}$ non-projected.

Finally, for $r=0$ the parity is locally constant ([FKP, §2.6] + Mumford/Cornalba; `paper.md` §2.4),
so $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{2;1,0}=\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{+}_{2;1,0}\sqcup
\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{-}_{2;1,0}$, and a disjoint union is projected iff every component is
(a retraction on the union restricts to each component, and conversely retractions on components
assemble). Hence $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{2;1,0}$ is non-projected. $\square$

**Proposition 3.2.** *$\overline{\mathfrak S}_{2;0,0}=\overline{\mathfrak M}_2$ is non-projected,
although its odd parity component $\overline{\mathfrak M}{}^{-}_2$ is split.*

*Proof.* K4 = [CN, Thm 1.1(2)] gives $\omega_2(\overline{\mathfrak M}{}^{+}_2)\ne0$, hence
$\overline{\mathfrak M}{}^{+}_2$ non-projected; the same disjoint-union argument as in Prop. 3.1 then
makes the whole $\overline{\mathfrak M}_2$ non-projected. K4 = [CN, Thm 1.1(1)] gives
$\overline{\mathfrak M}{}^{-}_2$ split. $\square$

**Theorem 3.3 (combined threshold).** *For every $g\ge2$, every $n\ge0$ and every $r\ge0$, the stack
$\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$ is non-projected. For $r=0$, the even parity component
$\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{+}_{g;n,0}$ is non-projected for every $g\ge2$ and every $n\ge0$.*

*Proof.* Partition the region.

* $g\ge3$, any $(n,r)$: K5 (`paper.md` Cor. 8.5 / Cor. 8.3(A1) for the even component).
* $g=2$, $n+2r\ge2$: K5 (`paper.md` Cor. 8.5; Cor. 8.3(A2) gives the even component when $r=0$,
  and Thm 8.4 gives the whole stack when $r\ge1$).
* $g=2$, $n+2r\le1$: since $2r$ is even, $n+2r\le1$ forces $r=0$ and $n\in\{0,1\}$. These are
  Prop. 3.2 ($n=0$) and Prop. 3.1 ($n=1$), and in both cases the even component is the one shown
  non-projected.

The union of the three cases is all of $g\ge2$. $\square$

> **Remark 3.4 (what this does and does not do to `paper.md`).**
> 1. Corollary 8.5 of `paper.md` is a *sufficient* condition and is not falsified by Theorem 3.3.
>    What is falsified is the implicit reading of it as a threshold: `paper.md` §1.5(3) and §10.6 list
>    $(g,n,r)=(2,1,0)$ as *"completely open here"*, and §9.2 discusses only why the method cannot
>    reach it. In fact the even component of that stack was already settled, in 2013, by [DW, Thm 1.2]
>    plus [DW, Remark 1.4]. The same holds for $(2,0,0)$ via [CN, Thm 1.1(2)].
> 2. The correct statement of what is *new* in `paper.md` at $g=2$ is therefore narrower than the box
>    suggests, and the correct statement of what is *true* is wider. Both should be said.
> 3. Nothing here touches $g\ge3$, where `paper.md` is strictly stronger than everything published
>    (no other source reaches a compactified punctured stack at all).
> 4. Theorem 3.3 leaves the **odd** parity component at $g=2$ with $n\ge1$ genuinely open:
>    $\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{-}_{2;n,0}$ for $n\ge1$. At $n=0$ it is split by [CN]; `paper.md`
>    §9.2 shows its method reaches only the even component at $g=2$; and [DW] states verbatim *"We do
>    not resolve the question of projectedness of $\mathfrak M_{g,n}$ for the case of even $g$, odd
>    spin structure, and $n\ge1$."* So all three sources stop at exactly the same wall, and the wall
>    is a real one.

## 4. The first split case of odd rank $2$: $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;4,0}$

Sections 1–3 used no computation. This section contains the one genuinely new calculation of the
attempt: the smallest stack to which neither Lemma 1.1 nor Theorem 3.3 applies,
$$\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;4,0},\qquad \dim=(3\cdot0-3+4\mid 2\cdot0-2+4)=(1\mid 2),$$
is **split**. Because $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal T_-=2$ here, K2 makes splitness *equivalent* to
$\omega_2=0$, so the computation is not merely sufficient — it is the whole question.

### 4.1 The bosonic truncation is a $\mu_2$-gerbe over a $\mu_2$-root stack

**Lemma 4.1.** *Let $X:=\overline S_{0;4,0}=(\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;4,0})_{\rm bos}$, the stack of
stable spin curves of genus $0$ with four labelled NS punctures. Let $\mathcal X:=X_{\rm rig}$
be its rigidification along the generic automorphism $-1$ of the spin sheaf. Then*
$$\mathcal X\ \cong\ \sqrt[2]{\big(\mathbb P^1_\lambda,\ [0]+[1]+[\infty]\big)},$$
*the $\mu_2$-root stack of $\overline M_{0,4}=\mathbb P^1_\lambda$ at its three boundary points, and
$X\to\mathcal X$ is a $\mu_2$-gerbe.*

*Proof.* Normalise $p_1=0$, $p_2=1$, $p_3=\infty$, $p_4=\lambda$; then $\overline M_{0,4}=\mathbb P^1_\lambda$
is a fine moduli scheme (four labelled points on $\mathbb P^1$ have no nontrivial automorphisms).

*Fibres.* Over $\lambda\notin\{0,1,\infty\}$ the curve is $C=\mathbb P^1$, $\omega_C=\mathcal O(-2)$,
and a spin structure is a line bundle $L$ with $L^{\otimes2}\cong\omega_C$; since
$\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1)=\mathbb Z$ is torsion-free, $L=\mathcal O(-1)$ is the unique solution,
and the isomorphism $L^{\otimes2}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\omega_C$ is unique up to
$\mathbb C^\times$, absorbable by rescaling $L$. The automorphisms of $(C,L,\phi)$ fixing the four
points are therefore exactly $\{\pm1\}$ acting on $L$. Over $\lambda\in\{0,1,\infty\}$ the stable curve
is $C'\cup_q C''$, two copies of $\mathbb P^1$ meeting in one node, with two of the $p_i$ on each. By K6
the spin sheaf is $j'_*L'\oplus j''_*L''$ with $L'^{\otimes2}\cong\omega_{C'}$ and
$L''^{\otimes2}\cong\omega_{C''}$ *untwisted*, i.e. $L'=L''=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)$, again unique.
So the map $X\to\overline M_{0,4}$ is bijective on points, and the coarse space of $X$ is $\mathbb P^1_\lambda$.

*Automorphisms.* Over the interior, $\operatorname{Aut}=\mu_2$. Over a boundary point, each branch
carries three special points ($q$ plus two punctures), so the underlying pointed curve is rigid, while
the spin sheaf admits the independent rescalings $(\alpha,\beta)$ with $\alpha^2=\beta^2=1$; hence
$\operatorname{Aut}=\mu_2\times\mu_2$. Rigidifying the diagonal $\mu_2$ (the one present everywhere)
leaves a residual $\mu_2$ at each of the three boundary points and trivial stabiliser elsewhere.

*Action on the smoothing parameter.* Let $t$ be the smoothing parameter of the node in
$\overline M_{0,4}$ (so $t$ is a local coordinate at $\lambda=0$, $\lambda=1$, $\lambda^{-1}=0$
respectively), and $\tau$ the smoothing parameter of the *spin* curve. Then $\tau^2=t$, and the
residual automorphism $(\alpha,\beta)=(-1,+1)$ acts by $\tau\mapsto-\tau$. Equivalently, in K8's
notation, the normal bundle of the boundary divisor $B\subset X$ is
$\mathcal O(\Delta_{\rm NS})|_B\cong\Psi_1^{-1}\Psi_2^{-1}$, whereas the normal bundle of
$\Delta\subset\overline M$ is $\psi_1^{-1}\psi_2^{-1}=(\Psi_1^{-1}\Psi_2^{-1})^{\otimes2}$ — the
displayed square being K8's $\Psi_i^{\otimes2}\cong p_i^*L^{\otimes2}=p_i^*\omega_C=\psi_i$ for an NS
puncture. So $N_B^{\otimes2}\cong N_\Delta|_B$: the map $X\to\overline M_{0,4}$ is ramified of index
exactly $2$ along the boundary.

*Conclusion.* $\mathcal X$ is a tame stacky curve with coarse space $\mathbb P^1$, trivial stabiliser
off $\{0,1,\infty\}$ and $\mu_2$ acting by $\tau\mapsto-\tau$ at each of those three points. By the
local structure theorem for tame stacky curves this is the $\mu_2$-root stack at the three points.
$\square$

> **[GAP-1]** The step *"$\tau^2=t$ and $(-1,+1)$ acts by $\tau\mapsto-\tau$"* is imported, not proved
> here. It is the classical ramification statement for Cornalba's compactified spin moduli, and it is
> **equivalent** to K8 as displayed. I have verified the equivalence but not re-derived K8 from
> [FKP] first principles. Note, however, that §4.3 below produces a section of $\det\mathcal T_-^\vee$
> whose local order at $\lambda=0$ is $\lambda^{-1/2}$; a half-integral order is *impossible* on a
> scheme, so the computation independently forces the stacky structure with even ramification. In that
> sense [GAP-1] is confirmed a posteriori, though only up to "ramification index divisible by $2$".

**Numerical consequences.** For the $\mu_2$-root stack at $k=3$ points of $\mathbb P^1$,
$$\deg\omega_{\mathcal X}=\deg\omega_{\mathbb P^1}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(1-\tfrac12\Big)=-2+\tfrac32=-\tfrac12,
\qquad \deg\mathcal T_{\mathcal X}=+\tfrac12 ,$$
and each stacky point $\mathcal D_i$ has $\deg\mathcal O(\mathcal D_i)=\tfrac12$.

### 4.2 The obstruction sheaf has weight $0$, so the gerbe is invisible

**Lemma 4.2.** *$\mathcal T_X\otimes\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee$ is pulled back from $\mathcal X$, and*
$$H^1\big(X,\ \mathcal T_X\otimes\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee\big)\ \cong\
H^1\big(\mathcal X,\ \mathcal T_{\mathcal X}\otimes\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee\big).$$

*Proof.* Coherent sheaves on a $\mu_2$-gerbe $\rho:X\to\mathcal X$ decompose into weight-$0$ and
weight-$1$ parts, and $R\rho_*$ kills the weight-$1$ part while being the identity on the weight-$0$
part; so it suffices to check that the sheaf has weight $0$.

$\mu_2$ is finite étale, so $\mathcal T_X=\rho^*\mathcal T_{\mathcal X}$: weight $0$.

By K7 and relative duality, $\mathcal T_-^\vee=\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2
=\pi_*\big(\omega_\pi\otimes\mathcal L(\mathcal D)\big)=\pi_*\big(\mathcal L^{\otimes3}(\mathcal D)\big)$,
where $\pi:\mathcal C\to X$ is the universal curve, $\mathcal L$ the universal spin sheaf,
$\mathcal D=\sum_{i=1}^4[\sigma_i]$, and $\omega_\pi=\mathcal L^{\otimes2}$. The generic automorphism
$-1$ acts on $\mathcal L$ by $-1$, hence on $\mathcal L^{\otimes3}$ by $(-1)^3=-1$: $\mathcal T_-^\vee$
has weight $1$. Therefore $\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee$ has weight $1+1=2\equiv0 \pmod 2$. $\square$

*(Consistency: rank $\mathcal T_-^\vee=h^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-3)(D))=h^0(\mathcal O(1))=2$, as it
must be, $2g-2+n+r=2$.)*

### 4.3 The determinant: $\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee\cong\mathcal T_{\mathcal X}$

Over the interior, sections of $\mathcal T_-^\vee$ are "$\tfrac32$-differentials with at worst simple
poles at the punctures": writing $\varphi=f(z)\,(dz)^{3/2}$ on $C=\mathbb P^1_z$ with
$p=(0,1,\infty,\lambda)$, the conditions are that $f$ have at worst simple poles at $0,1,\lambda$, no
other poles, and $f(z)=O(z^{-2})$ as $z\to\infty$ (because $(dz)^{3/2}=\pm w^{-3}(dw)^{3/2}$ for
$w=1/z$, so a simple pole at $w=0$ means $\operatorname{ord}_w f\ge2$). Partial fractions give
$f=\frac{A}{z}+\frac{B}{z-1}+\frac{C}{z-\lambda}$ with $A+B+C=0$: a $2$-dimensional space, as expected.

Fix the rational frame
$$\varphi_1:=\frac{(dz)^{3/2}}{(z-1)(z-\lambda)},\qquad
\varphi_2:=\frac{(dz)^{3/2}}{z(z-1)(z-\lambda)},\qquad
s:=\varphi_1\wedge\varphi_2\ \in\ \Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee .$$
Both satisfy the conditions, and they are independent for every $\lambda\notin\{0,1,\infty\}$
($\varphi_1$ is regular at $z=0$, $\varphi_2$ is not). Hence **$s$ is a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic
frame over the interior**, and $s$ is $\mu_2$-invariant (the sign ambiguity in $(dz)^{3/2}$ enters
$\varphi_1\wedge\varphi_2$ squared), so it descends to $\mathcal X$. It remains to compute
$\operatorname{ord}(s)$ at the three boundary points. Throughout, $e_1,e_2$ denote a local frame of
$\mathcal T_-^\vee$ near the boundary point whose values at the boundary generate, respectively, the
$C'$- and $C''$-summand of
$$\mathcal T_-^\vee\big|_{\rm bdry}
=H^0\!\big(C',L'^{\otimes3}(\text{3 special pts})\big)\ \oplus\
H^0\!\big(C'',L''^{\otimes3}(\text{3 special pts})\big),$$
each summand being $H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-3+3))=H^0(\mathcal O)=\mathbb C$ by K6 (the node counts
as an NS puncture on each branch). Note $1+1=2=\operatorname{rank}$, so $\mathcal T_-^\vee$ is indeed
locally free across the boundary and this is a legitimate frame.

**(i) $\lambda\to0$** ($p_1=0$ and $p_4=\lambda$ collide; bubble coordinate $y=z/\lambda$, so
$q\leftrightarrow z=0\leftrightarrow y=\infty$).

On $C'=\mathbb P^1_z$ (special points $q=0$, $p_2=1$, $p_3=\infty$):
$\varphi_1\to\frac{(dz)^{3/2}}{z(z-1)}$, which has simple poles at $0,1$ and is $O(z^{-2})$ at
$\infty$: a valid nonzero limit. So $e_1=\lim\varphi_1$.

On $C''$: $z=\lambda y$, $(dz)^{3/2}=\lambda^{3/2}(dy)^{3/2}$, hence
$$\varphi_2=\frac{\lambda^{3/2}(dy)^{3/2}}{\lambda y\,(\lambda y-1)\,\lambda(y-1)}
=\lambda^{-1/2}\,\frac{(dy)^{3/2}}{y(y-1)(\lambda y-1)},
\qquad \lambda^{1/2}\varphi_2\ \longrightarrow\ -\frac{(dy)^{3/2}}{y(y-1)} ,$$
valid on $C''$ (special points $p_1=0$, $p_4=1$, $q=\infty$). So $e_2=\lim\lambda^{1/2}\varphi_2$, and
in the same chart $\varphi_1=\lambda^{1/2}\frac{(dy)^{3/2}}{(\lambda y-1)(y-1)}\to0$, confirming that
$\varphi_1$ has no $e_2$-component at $\lambda=0$. Therefore
$$s=\varphi_1\wedge\varphi_2=\lambda^{-1/2}\,(1+O(\lambda))\ e_1\wedge e_2,
\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{\lambda=0}(s)=-\tfrac12 .$$

**(ii) $\lambda\to1$** ($\mu:=\lambda-1$; $p_2=1$ and $p_4=\lambda$ collide; $y=(z-1)/\mu$).

Neither $\varphi_1$ nor $\varphi_2$ has a limit on $C'$ (both acquire a double pole at $z=1=q$), but
$$\varphi_1-\varphi_2=\frac{(dz)^{3/2}}{z(z-\lambda)}\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{(dz)^{3/2}}{z(z-1)}=:e_1 ,$$
valid on $C'$ (special points $p_1=0$, $q=1$, $p_3=\infty$). On $C''$: $z-1=\mu y$,
$z-\lambda=\mu(y-1)$, so
$$\varphi_1=\frac{\mu^{3/2}(dy)^{3/2}}{\mu y\cdot\mu(y-1)}=\mu^{-1/2}\frac{(dy)^{3/2}}{y(y-1)},
\qquad e_2=\lim \mu^{1/2}\varphi_1=\frac{(dy)^{3/2}}{y(y-1)} ,$$
valid on $C''$ (special points $p_2=0$, $p_4=1$, $q=\infty$). Hence
$\varphi_1=\mu^{-1/2}(e_2+O(\mu))$ and $\varphi_2=\varphi_1-(e_1+O(\mu))$, so
$$s=\varphi_1\wedge\varphi_2=\big(\mu^{-1/2}e_2\big)\wedge(-e_1)+\cdots=\mu^{-1/2}(1+O(\mu))\,e_1\wedge e_2,
\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{\lambda=1}(s)=-\tfrac12 .$$

**(iii) $\lambda\to\infty$** ($\nu:=1/\lambda$; $p_3=\infty$ and $p_4=\lambda$ collide; $w=1/z$,
$y=w/\nu$).

On $C'=\mathbb P^1_z$ (special points $p_1=0$, $p_2=1$, $q=\infty$): from
$\frac1{z-\lambda}=-\nu\big(1+\nu z+\cdots\big)$,
$$\varphi_2=-\nu\,\frac{(dz)^{3/2}}{z(z-1)}\big(1+O(\nu)\big),
\qquad e_1:=\lim\big(-\nu^{-1}\varphi_2\big)=\frac{(dz)^{3/2}}{z(z-1)} ,$$
valid on $C'$ ($O(z^{-2})$ at $q=\infty$). By contrast $-\nu^{-1}\varphi_1\to\frac{(dz)^{3/2}}{z-1}$,
which is only $O(z^{-1})$ at $q$, hence **not** a section — so $\varphi_1$ must live on the bubble.
Indeed, with $z=1/(\nu y)$, $dz=-\frac{dy}{\nu y^2}$, $(dz)^{3/2}=\pm\nu^{-3/2}y^{-3}(dy)^{3/2}$,
$z-1=\frac{1-\nu y}{\nu y}$, $z-\lambda=\frac{1-y}{\nu y}$:
$$\varphi_1=\nu^{-3/2}y^{-3}(dy)^{3/2}\cdot\frac{\nu^2y^2}{(1-\nu y)(1-y)}
=\nu^{1/2}\,\frac{(dy)^{3/2}}{y(1-y)(1-\nu y)},\qquad
e_2:=\lim\nu^{-1/2}\varphi_1=\frac{(dy)^{3/2}}{y(1-y)},$$
valid on $C''$ (special points $p_3=0$, $p_4=1$, $q=\infty$). In the same chart
$\varphi_2=\nu y\,\varphi_1=O(\nu^{3/2})$, so $-\nu^{-1}\varphi_2\to 0$ on the bubble, confirming that
$\varphi_2$ has no $e_2$-component. Therefore
$$s=\varphi_1\wedge\varphi_2=\big(\nu^{1/2}e_2\big)\wedge\big(-\nu e_1\big)(1+\cdots)
=\nu^{3/2}(1+O(\nu))\,e_1\wedge e_2,\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{\lambda=\infty}(s)=+\tfrac32 .$$

**Proposition 4.3.** *$\deg_{\mathcal X}\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee=\tfrac12$. In fact*
$$\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee\ \cong\ \mathcal T_{\mathcal X}\ =\ \omega_{\mathcal X}^{-1} .$$

*Proof.* $s$ is a rational section with no zeros or poles in the interior, so
$$\deg\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee=\sum_{\lambda\in\{0,1,\infty\}}\operatorname{ord}_\lambda(s)
=-\tfrac12-\tfrac12+\tfrac32=\tfrac12 .$$
(Equivalently, in the stacky uniformisers $\tau$ with $\tau^2=\lambda,\ \lambda-1,\ \nu$, the orders are
$-1,-1,+3$, each stacky point having degree $\tfrac12$.)

For the sharper statement, compare with the rational section $d\lambda$ of $\omega_{\mathcal X}$:
$\operatorname{div}_{\mathbb P^1}(d\lambda)=-2[\infty]$, and in the stacky uniformisers
$d\lambda=2\tau\,d\tau$ at $\lambda=0$ and $\lambda=1$, while $d\lambda=-d\nu/\nu^2=-2\tau^{-3}d\tau$ at
$\lambda=\infty$; so $\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal X}(d\lambda)$ has $\tau$-orders $(+1,+1,-3)$, total
degree $\tfrac12(1+1-3)=-\tfrac12=\deg\omega_{\mathcal X}$ (an independent check on §4.1). The
$\tau$-orders of $s$ are $(-1,-1,+3)$, exactly the negatives. Two line bundles on a stacky curve with
equal divisors of rational sections are isomorphic, whence
$\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee\cong\omega_{\mathcal X}^{-1}$, the isomorphism sending
$s\mapsto\partial_\lambda$. $\square$

> **Remark 4.4 (why Prop. 4.3 is a strong self-check).** The three local orders were computed in three
> different charts with three different degenerations and no symmetry was assumed; that they conspire
> to reproduce $-\operatorname{div}(d\lambda)$ point by point (not merely in total degree) is a
> three-fold coincidence unless the computation is right. It also makes the answer manifestly
> $S_3$-equivariant for the anharmonic action on $(\mathbb P^1_\lambda;0,1,\infty)$ induced by
> relabelling the punctures, which the frame $(\varphi_1,\varphi_2)$ certainly is not.

### 4.4 Vanishing of $\omega_2$, and splitness

**Theorem 4.5.** *$\omega_2(\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;4,0})=0$, and $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;4,0}$ is
**split** (in particular projected).*

*Proof.* By Lemma 4.2 and Prop. 4.3, the obstruction group is
$$H^1\big(X,\mathcal T_X\otimes\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee\big)
\cong H^1\big(\mathcal X,\ \mathcal T_{\mathcal X}\otimes\mathcal T_{\mathcal X}\big)
= H^1\big(\mathcal X,\ \omega_{\mathcal X}^{-2}\big).$$
$\mathcal X$ is a proper, smooth, connected, tame stacky curve, so Serre duality holds:
$H^1(\mathcal X,\mathcal L)^\vee\cong H^0(\mathcal X,\omega_{\mathcal X}\otimes\mathcal L^{-1})$. Here
$\omega_{\mathcal X}\otimes\mathcal L^{-1}=\omega_{\mathcal X}^{3}$, of degree
$3\cdot(-\tfrac12)=-\tfrac32<0$. A nonzero global section of a line bundle on an integral proper stacky
curve has effective divisor, hence non-negative degree; therefore
$H^0(\mathcal X,\omega_{\mathcal X}^{3})=0$ and $H^1(\mathcal X,\omega_{\mathcal X}^{-2})=0$.
Consequently $\omega_2=0$.

By K1, $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;4,0}$ is a smooth DM superstack, and
$\operatorname{rank}\mathcal T_-=2g-2+n+r=2$. K2 = [CN, Prop. 2.2] therefore upgrades $\omega_2=0$ to
splitness. $\square$

> **Remark 4.6 (robustness).** The conclusion survives large errors in §4.1 and §4.3. Writing
> $\mathcal L=\mathcal T_{\mathcal X}\otimes\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee$, the vanishing
> $H^1(\mathcal X,\mathcal L)=0$ needs only
> $$\deg\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee\ >\ 2\deg\omega_{\mathcal X}=-1 .$$
> We found $+\tfrac12$, a margin of $\tfrac32$. Even if the stacky structure of Lemma 4.1 were wrong
> and $\mathcal X$ were the bare $\mathbb P^1$ ($\deg\omega=-2$), the requirement would be
> $\deg\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee>-4$, which the computed value also satisfies. The only way to break
> Theorem 4.5 is for $\Lambda^2\mathcal T_-^\vee$ to be *much* more negative than computed.

> **Remark 4.7 (context).** $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;4,0}$ is the supermoduli space of the tree-level
> four-point NS amplitude — the very first place where superstring perturbation theory integrates over
> a nontrivial supermoduli space. Theorem 4.5 says the classical practice of integrating over the odd
> directions fibrewise is *legitimate there*, not merely convenient. Contrast Theorem 3.3.

## 5. The corrected picture, and what remains open

### 5.1 Everything of bosonic dimension $\le1$ is split

**Theorem 5.1.** *If $\dim_{\rm even}\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}=3g-3+n+2r\le1$, then
$\overline{\mathfrak S}_{g;n,2r}$ is split. Explicitly the stable triples with $3g-3+n+2r\le1$ are*
$$\underbrace{(0;3,0),\ (0;1,2)}_{\dim=(0\mid\ast)}\ \cup\
\underbrace{(0;4,0),\ (0;2,2),\ (0;0,4),\ (1;1,0)}_{\dim=(1\mid\ast)} ,$$
*and all six are split.*

*Proof.* Stability is $2g-2+n+2r>0$. If $g\ge2$ then $3g-3\ge3$ and $3g-3+n+2r\ge3$, so no triple
occurs. If $g=1$ then $n+2r\le1$ and stability forces $n+2r=1$, i.e. $(n,r)=(1,0)$. If $g=0$ then
$3\le n+2r\le4$, giving $(3,0),(1,1)$ and $(4,0),(2,1),(0,2)$.

Their odd ranks $d=2g-2+n+r$ are $1,0,2,1,0,1$ in the order displayed. All but $(0;4,0)$ have $d\le1$
and are split by Corollary 1.3; $(0;4,0)$ is split by Theorem 4.5. $\square$

> **Remark 5.2 ($(0;4,0)$ is the *only* case the degree method can reach).** The argument of §4 needs
> $\dim_{\rm even}=1$ (so that $H^1$ of a line bundle is controlled by its degree) **and**
> $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal T_-=2$ (so that K2 turns $\omega_2=0$ into splitness). These two demand
> $3g-3+n+2r=1$ and $2g-2+n+r=2$; subtracting, $g+r=0$, hence $g=r=0$ and $n=4$. So Theorem 4.5 is
> not one instance of a family that the method could continue — it exhausts the method. Everything
> beyond it needs a genuinely different idea.

### 5.2 The smooth genus-$0$ spaces are split, and this says nothing about their compactifications

**Proposition 5.3 (sketch).** *For every $n\ge3$ the open substack $\mathfrak S_{0;n,0}$ of smooth
genus-$0$ supercurves with $n$ NS punctures is split; concretely
$\mathfrak S_{0;n,0}\cong[\,V/(\mathbb Z/2)\,]$ with $V\subseteq\mathbb C^{n-3\mid n-2}$ open.*

*Sketch.* A genus-$0$ super Riemann surface without punctures is rigid: its deformation space is
$H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal T)\oplus H^1(\mathbb P^1,L^{-1})=H^1(\mathcal O(2))\oplus H^1(\mathcal O(1))=0$,
so every such SRS is $\mathbb P^{1\mid1}$, with automorphism group $OSp(1\mid2)$ of dimension
$(3\mid2)$. The moduli problem is therefore the quotient of the configuration superspace of $n$ NS
punctures $(z_i\mid\theta_i)$ in $\mathbb P^{1\mid1}$ by $OSp(1\mid2)$. Gauge-fix $z_1=0$, $z_2=1$,
$z_3=\infty$ using the even part (the $PGL_2$-stabiliser of three points is trivial), and
$\theta_1=\theta_2=0$ using the two odd generators. The residual coordinates are
$(z_4,\dots,z_n\mid\theta_3,\dots,\theta_n)$, of dimension $(n-3\mid n-2)$ as required, and the residual
gauge group is the $\mathbb Z/2$ acting by $\theta\mapsto-\theta$. This presentation is manifestly
split. $\square$

> **[GAP-2]** Proposition 5.3 is written at the level of a gauge-fixing argument. Making it a theorem
> requires checking that the $OSp(1\mid2)$-action on the configuration superspace is free modulo the
> residual $\mathbb Z/2$ and that the quotient is representable by the stated global chart. I have not
> done that, and nothing else in this file depends on Proposition 5.3.

**Why it does not help.** Proposition 5.3 gives splitness of the *open* part only, and Lemma 2.1 runs
in the wrong direction for that: openness transports projectedness *downwards*, never upwards. The
published record shows the gap is real, not merely a defect of technique:

* [DW, Remark 1.5], verbatim: *"For $n\gg g$, requiring the $n$ points to be distinct very likely
  kills the obstructions to splitness, **though we would expect the compactification of
  $\mathfrak M_{g,n}$ to be non-split**."*
* [CN, Thm 1.1(2)] / [FKP2]: $\overline{\mathfrak M}{}^{+}_2$ is non-projected, while no source claims
  non-projectedness of the smooth $\mathfrak M^{+}_2$ (the earliest published non-projectedness for
  unpunctured smooth curves is [DW, Thm 1.1] at $g\ge5$; $g\ge3$ is only *suspected* there).

So the entire content of the residual question lives at the boundary divisors, exactly where
Proposition 5.3 has nothing to say.

### 5.3 The residual open region

Collecting Theorem 3.3 (non-projected), Corollary 1.3 and Theorems 4.5, 5.1 (split):

| region | verdict | by |
|---|---|---|
| $g\ge2$, all $n,r$ | **non-projected** | Thm 3.3 |
| $2g-2+n+r\le1$ | **split** | Cor 1.3 |
| $(g;n,2r)=(0;4,0)$ | **split** | Thm 4.5 |
| component $\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{-}_{2;0,0}=\overline{\mathfrak M}{}^{-}_2$ | **split** | K4 |
| $g=1$, $n+r\ge2$ | **open** | — |
| $g=0$, $n+r\ge4$, $(n,r)\ne(4,0)$ | **open** | — |
| component $\overline{\mathfrak S}{}^{-}_{2;n,0}$, $n\ge1$ | **open** | — |

**Why both directions stall on the open region.** Let $d=2g-2+n+r$ and $D=3g-3+n+2r$.

1. *Towards splitness.* K2's criterion "$\omega_2=0\Rightarrow$ split" is available only when $d=2$.
   The locus $d=2$ inside the open region is
   $$g=0:\ (n,r)\in\{(3,1),(2,2),(1,3),(0,4)\},\qquad g=1:\ (n,r)\in\{(2,0),(1,1),(0,2)\}$$
   (the remaining member $g=2$, $(n,r)=(0,0)$ is [CN]'s theorem, and $g=0$, $(4,0)$ is Theorem 4.5).
   For each of these $D\ge2$, so $H^1$ of the obstruction sheaf is no longer controlled by a degree.
   The smallest are $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{1;2,0}$ and $\overline{\mathfrak S}_{0;3,2}$, both with
   $D=2$: **surfaces**. Computing $H^1$ of a line bundle on those two stacky surfaces is the concrete
   next task, and it is a finite amount of work, not a new idea.
   For $d\ge3$ even $\omega_2=0$ would not suffice: one would need the higher obstructions
   $\omega_4,\omega_6,\dots$ or a direct construction of a retraction.
2. *Towards non-projectedness.* Every published non-vanishing of $\omega_2$ — [DW], [DO], [W], [CN],
   and `paper.md` itself — is ultimately produced by restricting to a boundary or a gluing locus and
   exhibiting a component of $\omega_2$ proportional to a *positive* multiple of a class that survives.
   All of them need $g\ge2$ somewhere: `paper.md` needs (P1) $h\ge2$, whose failure at $h\le1$ is not
   a technical accident but the vanishing $H^1(C,L^{-1})=0$ recorded in `paper.md` Lemma 8.2(i);
   [DW]/[DO]/[W] need $g\ge2$, $g\ge5r+1$, $g\ge n+5r+3$ respectively. **[GAP-3]:** I found no
   mechanism at all that produces a nonzero $\omega_2$ from genus $\le1$ data, and I did not prove
   that none exists.
